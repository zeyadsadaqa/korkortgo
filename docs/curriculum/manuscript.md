**2026 review edition · Original checks 1 October; chapter additions checked 12–13 October 2026**

> **This material is a learning aid to help you understand Swedish driving theory. It is not an official source and cannot replace official information from Trafikverket and Transportstyrelsen. Always consult their current guidance and the applicable rules.**

This book is for English-speaking learners preparing for ordinary Swedish category B driving. It connects rules with decisions you can explain and practise. It does not grant permission to drive, replace mandatory training or supervised practice, reproduce official examination questions, or guarantee a test result.

### How to use the book

Begin with modules 1–3. They establish personal readiness, vehicle preparation and road interpretation. Use modules 4–7 to apply that foundation in traffic and changing conditions. Modules 8–9 develop responsibility for the vehicle, its load and the journey's impact. Module 10 brings the learning together.

Each lesson contains a goal, an explanation, an original worked situation, a knowledge check with its answer, and a short reminder. After each module, write a reflection and use it to plan your next study or supervised practice session. Explain the answer before reading it; then change one condition in the example and consider whether your decision would change.

**Requirements** describe applicable duties or conditions, with official references. **Official recommendations/advice** describe the named authority's guidance. **Learning approaches, worked decisions and review guides** are original teaching material. They help you apply the principles; they are not additional laws. English explanations are written for learning and do not replace the Swedish legal wording.

References appear beside factual material. Trafikverket and Transportstyrelsen provide the main guidance. Official legislation and other public authorities supply details missing from the main agency guidance. Selected manufacturer handbooks supply missing technical cautions and are listed separately. The lessons identify source limits and distinguish recommendations, research and teaching inferences from legal duties. Use the handbook for the actual vehicle; a generic example is not its operating procedure.

Original licensing, examination and vehicle checks are dated 1 October 2026; added passages have their own checks on 12–13 October. Check current official information before using a time-sensitive rule. Hypothetical numbers in examples are identified as such and are not measurements of your vehicle or personal performance.

## 1. Choose to drive responsibly

Driving begins with a choice: whether this person, this vehicle and this journey are ready to go together. Sweden’s Vision Zero (Nollvisionen) aims for no one to be killed or seriously injured in traffic. The choices taught here contribute to that aim. [Trafikverket: Vision Zero][vision-zero] Learning rules matters, but so does recognising when a different plan is safer. This module helps you build both habits.

### M01-L01 · Your route into driving

**Your goal:** Build a personal preparation checklist covering entitlement, permits, supervised practice, risk training and examinations.

**Requirements.** For an ordinary new category B licence, you need a valid learner permit, permanent residence in Sweden or at least six months of study here, the minimum age of 18, valid car risk training, and successful theory and practical tests. Holding another EEA licence affects the application route. Category B normally covers a passenger car or light goods vehicle with a registered maximum weight of no more than 3,500 kg; trailers are explained in module 8. Check any conditions on your entitlement, including an automatic-transmission restriction. [Transportstyrelsen: category B][b]

Private practice requires an approved supervisor for the particular learner. The learner normally needs a group 1 permit. Practice in a passenger car can begin at 16; the minimum is 17 years and 6 months for practice with a light goods vehicle or a light trailer. The supervisor is legally treated as the driver, must carry proof of approval, and the practice sign must be clearly visible from behind. Exceptions concerning an existing entitlement must be checked against your own circumstances. [Transportstyrelsen: category B][b]

**Current changes, checked 1 October 2026.** The introductory-course requirement for private practice ended on 1 August 2026. Supervisor approval still applies. This change does not remove mandatory risk training. A theory test passed on or after 19 August 2026 is valid for one year. Tests passed before that date retain the earlier rules; Trafikverket describes the ordinary earlier validity as four months. Check your own result and any approved extension rather than adding a year yourself. [Supervisor requirements][supervisor] · [Risk training][risk] · [Test validity amendment][validity] · [Trafikverket: B tests][test]

Risk training has two parts: the first covers alcohol, drugs, fatigue and other risky behaviour; the second covers speed, safety and differing road/weather conditions, including slippery-road training. It must be completed before the tests and remains valid until the licence is obtained or for at most five years. Car and motorcycle risk training are not interchangeable. [Transportstyrelsen: risk training][risk]

**Build your checklist.** Record your permit, supervisor approval if practising privately, entitlement conditions, risk-training status, test preparation and any support application. Use actual expiry dates from your records. Schedule practice around skills you need to improve, not only around an available test slot.

**Worked decision.** Noor plans to practise privately in September 2026. Her relative says no approvals are needed because a course was abolished. Noor checks the current supervisor page and waits for supervisor approval. The removed course and the retained approval are different requirements.

**Knowledge check.** Does a theory pass from July 2026 automatically become valid for a year? **Answer:** No. The amendment has transitional rules for tests taken before 19 August. Confirm the applicable expiry in the official result information.

**Remember:** Plan the administrative route and the learning route together.

### M01-L02 · Am I ready today?

**Your goal:** Decide whether to drive when tiredness, illness, medication, alcohol or drugs may affect you, and explain a safer alternative.

**Requirement.** You must not drive when illness, exhaustion, alcohol, other substances or another condition prevents safe control. A legal alcohol threshold is not a guarantee that you are capable of driving safely. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §1][traffic-law]

Trafikverket states that the blood-alcohol limit for drink-driving is 0.2 per mille. For an aggravated offence, factors include at least 1.0 per mille, substantial impairment, or driving that creates a clear traffic danger. Non-prescribed narcotics are subject to a zero-tolerance rule. Properly prescribed medication does not make impaired driving acceptable. [Trafikverket: drink-driving law][drink-law] · [Alcohol, drugs and medication][alcohol]

Alcohol can affect judgement, attention, vision, reactions and coordination. Feeling normal does not measure those effects. Performance can remain impaired the next day. Do not use a fixed number of hours, a shower, coffee or a calculation as a personal clearance to drive. Ask your prescriber or pharmacist about medicines and driving; do not change a prescribed treatment simply to make a journey. [Trafikverket: effects of alcohol and medicines][alcohol]

**Learning approach.** Before taking the keys, ask: Can I stay alert? Can I see and move well enough? Has anything changed since I last drove? Can I concentrate on several developing situations? If the answers are uncertain, make a different travel plan and resolve the uncertainty first. Medical eligibility and day-to-day readiness are related but different: having a licence does not mean you are fit for every journey. [Medical requirements][medical]

**Medical explanation · Sleep and awareness.** Microsleep (mikrosömn) is a brief, unintended episode of sleep. You do not choose it or reliably prevent it by deciding to try harder. For a deliberately hypothetical two-second lapse at a constant 90 km/h, distance = speed × time: 90 ÷ 3.6 = 25 metres per second; 25 × 2 = 50 metres travelled while asleep. Two seconds is an example, not a prediction of how long a microsleep lasts. [CDC/NIOSH: microsleeps][microsleep]

**Official safety advice · Replace missing sleep with sleep.** Adequate sleep is the fundamental remedy for sleep deprivation. Coffee or an energy drink may briefly make you feel more alert, but does not replace the sleep you need; serious sleep deprivation can still lead to microsleep. Music, an open window or willpower should not be treated as a clearance to drive: they do not provide the missing sleep. Do not start when you cannot stay awake; if sleepiness develops, stop somewhere safe and arrange rest or another way to travel. See M07-L03 for planning around fatigue on a journey. [NHTSA: drowsy driving][drowsy-driving]

**Medical explanation · Stimulation is not safe judgement.** Stimulant misuse, for example involving amphetamine or cocaine, can produce overconfidence while judgement and concentration are impaired. Some stimulant effects can reduce the feeling of fatigue or drowsiness: tiredness may be masked rather than the need for sleep removed. Feeling energetic is therefore not proof of safe driving ability. Effects vary with substance, dose and individual circumstances; this does not mean every prescribed stimulant treatment has the same effect. Ask your prescriber about your treatment and driving. [Swedish Government: stimulant confidence and judgement][stimulant-confidence] · [NHTSA: stimulant effects][stimulant-fatigue]

**Practice example.** Lina slept too little before an evening journey. She suggests an energy drink and loud music; her friend suggests a stimulant to make her feel confident. The missing sleep remains unresolved, and a stronger feeling of alertness cannot certify safe judgement. She postpones the drive or arranges an alert driver, and makes time for adequate sleep before driving again.

**Worked decision.** A driver has taken a new medicine and feels unusually drowsy. A passenger offers to keep talking to help them stay awake. The safer choice is to arrange another driver or transport and ask for medication advice. Conversation does not resolve the impairment.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Is driving automatically acceptable if a medicine was prescribed correctly? (2) How does microsleep differ from choosing to close your eyes? (3) A sleep-deprived driver feels energetic and unusually confident after taking a stimulant. Has the missing sleep been remedied, and does that feeling establish safe judgement? **Answer:** (1) No. The driver must still be capable of driving safely. The prescription exception to the narcotics rule does not excuse unsafe impairment. (2) Microsleep is a brief unintended sleep episode, not a deliberate action. (3) No. Adequate sleep addresses sleep deprivation; stimulation can mask fatigue and produce overconfidence without safe judgement.

**Remember:** Decide fitness before deciding convenience.

### M01-L03 · Pressure is a driving risk

**Your goal:** Identify how peers, impulses, confidence and personal circumstances can change a decision, and rehearse a refusal or alternative.

The official education syllabus asks learners to reflect on social influence, impulsive decisions, personal motives and their own limitations. These are skills to practise, not labels to attach to an age group or personality. [Category B syllabus, chapter 5][syllabus]

**Learning approach.** Separate the journey's purpose from the feeling of the moment. “Get everyone home” is a purpose. “Show that I can handle this bend quickly” is a different motive. When a passenger's request changes your speed, following distance or willingness to overtake, name the change before acting on it.

A useful personal boundary is a sentence you can use without debate: “I will stop somewhere safe before changing the route”; “I am not passing here”; or “We need another way home.” These are original rehearsal tools, not official scripts. Practise them before a difficult situation, and agree that passengers will help with navigation only when asked.

**Requirement.** You still owe care to other road users and must adapt speed to conditions. Cooperation does not authorise ignoring a traffic rule because another person is impatient. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 2 §1 and chapter 3 §14][traffic-law]

**Official policy aim · Vision Zero.** Nollvisionen means no one should be killed or seriously injured in traffic. It is not a promise that nobody will ever make a mistake. Trafikverket describes shared responsibility: road users must show consideration and judgement and follow traffic rules, while system designers have ultimate responsibility for the system’s safety. Your part includes choosing safe speed and margins and refusing a pressured manoeuvre; road and vehicle design also contribute. For example, staying behind a vehicle near a hidden crest helps protect people even when a passenger dislikes the delay. [Trafikverket: Vision Zero][vision-zero]

**Worked decision.** Friends laugh when Amir stays behind a slow vehicle on a road with a hidden crest. He recognises the urge to prove himself, maintains space and waits for a genuinely suitable opportunity. His decision protects the purpose of the trip: arriving safely. There is no need to “win” the interaction.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Which is better evidence of readiness: feeling confident, or repeatedly identifying hazards and responding safely during supervised practice? (2) Does Vision Zero aim only to reduce minor collisions, or to prevent deaths and serious injuries? **Answer:** (1) Observed performance. Confidence is useful only when it agrees with what you can actually do. (2) Its central aim is that no one should be killed or seriously injured in traffic. Safer driver choices contribute to that aim.

**Remember:** A decision can be socially uncomfortable and still be the correct driving decision.

### M01-L04 · Plan the journey before the ignition

**Your goal:** Compare route, departure time, travel mode and passenger choices for safety, accessibility and environmental impact.

Trafikverket recommends checking forecasts and traffic conditions, allowing time, taking breaks on longer trips and preparing supplies appropriate to the season. The education syllabus also asks you to consider whether a car is the right means of transport and how passengers or timing affect a journey. [Journey advice][planning] · [Syllabus, chapters 4–5][syllabus]

**Learning approach.** Plan a usable journey rather than an ideal arrival time. Check where you can rest, refuel or charge as appropriate to the vehicle, and stop legally at the destination. Arrange restraints and space for passengers before departure. If the route has unfamiliar junctions or restrictions, identify them while parked.

Compare realistic alternatives. Walking, cycling or public transport may suit some journeys; a car may be necessary for others. Consider access needs, luggage, the return trip and the conditions at the time you will actually travel. A plan that depends on rushing after a late appointment is already fragile.

**Worked decision.** A learner and supervisor can practise on a familiar route in daylight or travel through an unfamiliar town during a forecast snow shower. They choose daylight practice today and plan a later lesson for more demanding conditions with suitable preparation. This is staged learning, not an assumption that difficult conditions can always be avoided.

**Knowledge check.** You are delayed before leaving. What should change first: the arrival expectation or the safety margin? **Answer:** The arrival expectation. Inform the people waiting, choose a different mode or postpone if necessary. A late start does not change the rules or stopping space needed.

**Remember:** Keep a fallback plan that does not depend on taking extra risk.

### M01-R · Review and self-assessment

**Scenario:** You intend to practise after a long workday, collect a friend and reach an appointment on time. Your supervisor is approved, but the road forecast has worsened.

Explain what you would check before leaving, which circumstances could make you cancel, and how you would respond if your friend urged you to hurry. **Review guide:** Include legal permission, actual fitness, vehicle/passenger readiness, conditions and a workable alternative. Permission to practise is only one part of the decision.

Write one decision you are uncertain about, its possible consequences, and the official reference or supervised practice activity that would help. Keep the entry and revisit it after your next practice session. [Syllabus self-assessment, chapters 4–5 §3][syllabus]

## 2. Prepare a car that is ready

A vehicle check connects what you notice with what you do next. Finding a defect is useful only if you recognise its significance, avoid an unsafe journey and obtain the right help.

### M02-L01 · Know what the controls tell you

**Your goal:** Explain the purpose of core controls, warning indicators, braking and steering systems, and identify when further instructions are needed.

The practical test can include checking steering, brakes, tyres, lighting, fluids and warning systems. You should be able to explain why you check them and propose action if you find a problem. Before moving, adjust the seat, head restraint, steering wheel, mirrors and belt. [Trafikverket: safety check][practical]

**Learning approach.** While parked, locate the service brake, parking brake, transmission controls, indicators, lighting controls, wipers, washers, demisting and hazard lights. Explain what each control is for, then demonstrate it with your supervisor. “I can find it” and “I can use it without losing attention” are different learning stages.

The service brake is used to reduce speed and stop; the parking brake helps secure a stationary vehicle. Steering changes the vehicle's direction, but the tyres and road must provide the forces needed to follow that direction. Braking and steering share the available friction: turning the wheel is not proof that the car can follow the intended path. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§14 and 50][traffic-law] · [VTI friction report, section 4.3][friction]

**Vehicle-specific boundary.** Control layouts, warning meanings and system checks differ. Use the instructions for the actual vehicle before trying an unfamiliar function. This material does not prescribe a universal brake-servo test, fluid-opening procedure or response to every dashboard symbol. An unexplained warning is a reason to establish what is wrong before treating the car as ready.

**Technical explanation · Brakes and electrical protection.** Separate brake circuits provide a reserve: if one circuit fails, the remaining circuit can still provide some braking. This reduces the chance of losing all braking through one circuit fault. Remaining braking may be weaker; it is not permission to continue normally. The technical requirements distinguish the surviving capability from full service-brake performance. [Transportstyrelsen: brake circuits][brake-circuits]

**ABS in an emergency.** Anti-lock braking helps prevent wheel locking and can preserve steering control. During an emergency stop with ABS, keep firm, continuous pressure on the brake pedal and steer as necessary; do not pump the pedal. Pulsation or vibration through the pedal during ABS operation can be normal feedback as the system regulates braking. This differs from a changed pedal feel in ordinary braking. ABS does not guarantee a shorter stop or create grip that the tyres lack. [UK Government: ABS instruction, Rule 120][abs-driving] · [Ford: normal ABS feedback, 2011 Explorer][abs-feedback]

**Recognise a symptom, then act.** The table gives possibilities, not diagnoses. A sinking pedal can suggest internal leakage, although some servo/pump behaviour can resemble it. Air can cause hydraulic pedal sponginess. Obtain professional assessment for an unexplained change. [Transportstyrelsen: brake inspection][brake-inspection] · [Northern Ireland government: vehicle faults][vehicle-faults]

| Symptom | Possible cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Pulsating pedal during an emergency ABS stop | Normal ABS operation | Keep firm continuous pressure; steer as needed. |
| Pedal slowly sinks under steady pressure | Hydraulic leakage or another brake fault | Do not start an unsafe journey; obtain professional help. |
| Hydraulic pedal feels unusually springy or spongy | Air in the brake system | Have it checked before moving; do not rely on normal braking. |
| Steering vibrates at certain speeds | Wheel imbalance; other faults are possible | Have tyres, wheels and steering assessed; see M02-L02. |

**What a fuse does.** A fuse protects an electrical circuit by interrupting excessive current during an overload or short circuit. Investigate faults under the actual vehicle's instructions. [Transportstyrelsen: fuse protection principle][fuse-protection]

**Worked decision.** Leila starts a borrowed car and sees a warning she cannot identify. Instead of driving to see whether it disappears, she stays parked, consults the vehicle instructions and seeks help. The lesson is to resolve uncertainty, not to guess from a warning's colour alone.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Does a familiar selector guarantee familiar operation? (2) Why separate brake circuits? (3) What should you do when the pedal pulsates during emergency ABS braking? (4) What might a sinking or unusually spongy pedal suggest? (5) What does a fuse protect against? **Answer:** (1) No; establish operation while stationary. (2) A remaining circuit can retain some braking after one fails, with reduced capability. (3) Keep firm continuous pressure and steer as needed; normal ABS pulsation is not a reason to pump. (4) Sinking can suggest a hydraulic leak or fault; sponginess can suggest air. Obtain professional assessment before an unsafe journey. (5) Excessive current; the fuse interrupts the circuit.

**Remember:** Learn a function, its limitations and the appropriate response—not only its button.

### M02-L02 · A check before moving

**Your goal:** Organise a safety check of tyres, lights, visibility and vehicle condition, and distinguish a defect from a reason to seek help.

**Learning approach.** Work around the car, then through the driving position. Look for damaged tyres, an unusual stance, leaks or obstructions; confirm that windows and mirrors permit a useful view. Check required lights and indicators, and ask for help where a second person is useful. Check washers and wipers before you need them in poor weather. Use the actual vehicle's instructions for fluid levels and service checks. These groups reflect the official practical-test safety check; they are not a guarantee that a quick walkaround detects every defect. [Trafikverket: safety check][practical]

**Requirements.** Tyres must be suitable for the vehicle, including load capacity and applicable speed capability. The registration information and approved vehicle requirements matter when changing tyre sizes. Do not assume any wheel that physically fits is suitable. [Transportstyrelsen: tyre requirements][tyres]

Required lighting must work and be used appropriately. Some cars can run daytime lights without rear lights; the driver must ensure suitable front, rear and side lighting is on in darkness, twilight or poor weather. A trailer's lighting must be on during travel. Automatic settings may need manual intervention. [Transportstyrelsen: vehicle lighting][lights]

**Technical explanation · Steering vibration.** Uneven mass distribution around a rotating wheel and tyre can cause steering vibration, sometimes most noticeable at particular speeds. Wheel imbalance is one possible cause, not a diagnosis: tyre damage and other wheel or steering defects may also be involved. Arrange assessment rather than testing the symptom by driving faster. [Northern Ireland government: wheel balance and vehicle faults][vehicle-faults]

**Vehicle instructions · A temporary spare.** Before using a temporary spare, read the actual car and spare-wheel instructions for maximum speed, distance or use restrictions, and required tyre pressure. Follow those limits and replace it with the normal wheel as instructed. Its pressure can differ from the road tyres. Volvo's 2019 V60 Twin Engine handbook gives one model's temporary-use, speed and pressure requirements; these cannot be assumed for every car. [Volvo: temporary spare, 2019 V60 Twin Engine][temporary-spare]

**Practice example.** Omar notices vibration on a journey and proposes fitting an unfamiliar temporary spare. He first obtains help to assess the symptom, then checks whether the spare is suitable and reads its limits. Changing a wheel does not establish the original cause. Handling and grip are developed in M07-L02. For hot-coolant precautions during other vehicle checks, see M10-L01.

**Worked decision.** A passenger notices that the trailer's rear lights are dark although the car's daytime lights are on. The driver checks the setting and connection before moving. “The dashboard looks normal” is not a check of the trailer lamps.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Is deep tread enough if a tyre has a defect? (2) What is one possible cause of steering vibration at certain speeds? (3) Which instructions must you check before using a temporary spare? **Answer:** (1) No; condition and suitability also matter. Have the defect assessed. (2) Wheel imbalance is one possibility; assessment is needed because other faults can cause vibration. (3) The actual vehicle/spare maximum speed, distance or use restrictions, and required pressure; no universal limit can be assumed.

**Remember:** Use a repeatable order, then take a specific action for each concern. Detailed winter requirements are in M07-L02.

### M02-L03 · Protect everyone inside

**Your goal:** Select suitable restraints for passengers and explain how belts, headrests and airbags work together.

**Requirements.** In an ordinary car journey, occupants must use an available belt-equipped place and wear the belt. Children under 135 cm normally need an appropriate child restraint. The driver must take appropriate measures to ensure passengers under 15 use the required protection. Limited exceptions exist, including specified short occasional journeys, and should not become a routine travel plan. [Seat-belt rules][belts] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 4 §§10–10d][traffic-law]

For a child aged three or more but shorter than 135 cm, an occasional short transport without an available child restraint is an exception only under the stated conditions: use the belt where possible and not the front seat of a passenger car or light goods vehicle. For a child under three, the exception is an occasional short taxi trip, also excluding the front seat. Plan with the correct restraint instead. [Transportstyrelsen: child rules][child-rules]

**Official recommendations.** Trafikverket recommends rear-facing travel until at least four to five years, preferably longer. Transportstyrelsen's advice says as long as possible, normally around four. These are recommendations, not a single legal birthday on which to turn the seat. Choose equipment that fits the child and car and stays within its approved size and weight limits. Reaching 135 cm does not automatically produce good adult-belt fit. [Trafikverket: children in cars][child-advice] · [Transportstyrelsen: child protection][child-rules]

Never place a rear-facing child restraint in front of an active passenger airbag. Trafikverket advises at least 140 cm in front of an active airbag unless the manufacturer recommends otherwise; Transportstyrelsen gives more conservative advice for children in restraints. Resolve seating and airbag compatibility using the vehicle and restraint instructions before travelling. An airbag supplements a belt; it does not replace one. Adjust the head restraint for the person occupying the seat. [Children in cars][child-advice] · [Airbags][airbags] · [Seats and head restraints][seats]

**Technical explanation · Head restraints.** In a rear impact, the seat and torso can move while the head lags behind. A correctly designed and adjusted head restraint helps limit damaging head movement relative to the torso and reduce neck-injury risk. Trafikverket advises positioning the top at the crown of the head and keeping the gap behind the head as small as possible. Adjust it for the occupant before driving; a comfort setting is not the same as a protective setting. A head restraint cannot guarantee that injury will be avoided. [Trafikverket: seats and head restraints][seats]

**Why children face backwards.** A young child's head is relatively heavy and the neck is vulnerable. In a frontal collision, a rear-facing seat supports the head and body together, takes up collision forces and distributes the load across the supported body. The neck does not have to restrain the heavy head on its own in the same way as when facing forwards. This is why the recommendations above concern protection, not only seat orientation. Follow the seat's size limits, fitting instructions and airbag compatibility. [Trafikverket: child head and neck protection][child-advice] · [Transportstyrelsen: rear-facing collision protection][child-rules]

**Controlled deformation.** Crumple zones are parts of a vehicle designed to deform progressively in a collision, absorbing energy while the passenger compartment's structure protects the occupants' space. Spreading the deceleration over a longer time reduces forces acting on the people inside. A car remaining visually undamaged is not the objective: designed deformation can be part of its protection. Belts and suitable child restraints still matter, and no structure makes every collision survivable. [Western Australia government: structure and crumple zones][crumple-zones]

**Practice example.** A family prepares a car for a child and an adult passenger. They check rear-facing seat fit and airbag compatibility, and adjust the adult's head restraint. After discussing crash protection, they recognise that the restraint, seat and deforming vehicle structure have different roles that work together.

**Worked decision.** A child reaches the legal height threshold, but the belt still lies badly. The adult checks an appropriate approved booster rather than treating the threshold as an instruction to discard it.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Is a rear-facing seat safe in front of an active airbag for a short trip? (2) What does a head restraint do? (3) Why does rear-facing travel protect a small child? (4) Why are crumple zones designed to deform? **Answer:** (1) No. Trip length does not make it safe. (2) It helps limit damaging head movement and reduce neck-injury risk, particularly in rear impacts. (3) The seat supports the relatively heavy head and body together and takes collision forces, protecting the vulnerable neck. (4) Controlled deformation absorbs energy and slows the compartment more gradually, reducing occupant forces.

**Remember:** Legal minimums, official recommendations and equipment compatibility answer different questions.

### M02-L04 · Move with control

**Your goal:** Plan observation and control for starting, stopping, reversing, hill starts and turning, and explain why supervised practice is needed.

**Requirements.** Before moving away from the edge, changing position or lane, you must ensure the movement can be made without danger or unnecessary obstruction. Turning around and reversing must be possible without danger or obstruction to other road users. Signal your intention where required, but a signal does not give you priority. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§43, 46 and 65–66][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Break a manoeuvre into preparation, observation, controlled movement and reassessment. Choose a place with enough room; check around the vehicle and along the intended path; move only while the space remains safe. Stop and reassess when the view changes. Looking once before starting is not continuous observation.

A hill start is a control task: prevent unintended movement while coordinating the actual vehicle's transmission, brakes and accelerator. Do not copy pedal sequences between cars without instruction. Reversing, parking and turning also need practical feedback about vehicle dimensions and the path followed by its corners. The official practical test may examine these skills, including effective braking. Practise braking exercises only in suitable, supervised conditions—not as an experiment in ordinary traffic. [Trafikverket: manoeuvring and braking][practical]

**Worked decision.** While reversing into a space, a driver loses sight of a person who was beside the car. The driver stops. The empty camera image is not enough to resolve where the person went; direct observation and an adequate overall view are still needed.

**Knowledge check.** If you have signalled and started moving, must another road user always make room? **Answer:** No. Your obligation to check safety remains throughout the manoeuvre.

**Remember:** Slow control gives you opportunities to observe and stop; it does not remove the need to look.

### M02-R · Review and self-assessment

**Scenario:** You will drive an unfamiliar car with a child passenger, then reverse out of a confined space.

Describe your preparation and the point at which you would stop and seek help. **Review guide:** Establish control operation, vehicle condition, restraint and airbag compatibility, visibility and a safe manoeuvre plan. Do not improvise a technical check or reverse into an uncertain space.

Record one control or check you can explain but cannot yet perform reliably. Arrange supervised practice and review the result. [Syllabus self-assessment, chapter 2 §3][syllabus]

## 3. Read the road before acting

Reading a road means combining instructions, movement, visibility and uncertainty. A sign tells you something important; it rarely tells you everything needed for the next decision.

### M03-L01 · Recognise what a sign asks

**Your goal:** Classify the main sign families and describe the decision each one prompts.

Start with purpose, then read the specific symbol and any supplementary panel. The official catalogue groups signs by function. Do not rely on colour alone: similar shapes can communicate different duties, and some important signs have distinctive shapes. [Transportstyrelsen: sign catalogue][signs]

| Family | What to establish | A useful driving question |
|---|---|---|
| A — warning | The nature of a hazard ahead | What might be hidden, and how much space do I need? |
| B — priority | A duty or relationship at a conflict point | Must I yield or stop, and to whom? |
| C — prohibition/restriction | What movement, vehicle or limit is restricted | Does this apply to my vehicle and journey? |
| D — mandatory instruction | The required route, side or permitted traffic | Which path must I take? |
| E — instruction | A road or area with a particular meaning or regime | Which rules begin or end here? |

These are learning questions, not substitutes for the individual sign descriptions. A warning of children, for example, does not assign a new numerical speed by itself; your speed must still allow safe control and a response to foreseeable hazards. A posted maximum speed is a ceiling, not a demand to maintain that speed. [Road Signs Regulation, chapter 2][sign-law] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§14–15][traffic-law]

**Requirements.** B1 means yield to vehicles on the crossing road or route. Make that intention clear early and proceed only without danger or obstruction. B2 requires a stop before entering the crossing road or track area. Stop at the stop line; without one, stop immediately before the signal or before entering the crossing road or track area, as applicable. A rolling pause does not meet a stop duty. [Road Signs Regulation, B1–B2][sign-law] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§5 and 19][traffic-law]

**Worked decision.** You see a warning sign for a narrow section. You reduce speed and look for oncoming traffic before reaching it. You then read any priority instruction at the narrow point. The warning alone did not decide who should pass first.

**Knowledge check.** Does seeing a warning sign tell you an exact safe speed? **Answer:** No. Assess the hazard, visibility, road surface and people present as well as the applicable limit.

**Remember:** Identify the instruction and turn it into an early action. The illustrated sign reference is prepared separately from this text.

### M03-L02 · Combine the instructions

**Your goal:** Interpret signs, panels, markings and signals together, including authorised directions.

**Requirements.** Follow the traffic instructions that apply to you. Where an instruction differs from a general traffic rule, the instruction governs. A steady traffic-light indication takes precedence over a yield or stop sign; a police officer's direction takes precedence over the rules and ordinary traffic instructions. This is not a rule that a green signal cancels every other restriction. Authorised guards also give traffic directions that must be followed. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 2 §§2–4][traffic-law]

For ordinary vehicle signals: red means stop; red and amber together still mean stop, with green approaching. Green permits movement, subject to other duties and space ahead. Amber means stop unless, when green changes to amber, you are already so close that stopping would be dangerous. Flashing amber requires particular caution; read the signs and applicable rules. Arrow signals apply to the direction indicated. [Road Signs Regulation, chapter 3 §§3–6][sign-law]

A red lane-control cross closes the lane; a green downward arrow allows its use; a diagonal amber arrow tells you to move in the indicated direction. Make any lane change safely. Signals for pedestrians or public transport are not automatically instructions for your car. [Road Signs Regulation, chapter 3 §§7–15][sign-law]

**Markings.** A broken centre or lane line identifies a boundary; it does not promise that overtaking is safe. A warning line indicates that crossing is unsuitable because of conditions and may precede a continuous line. Continuous lines and hatched areas have crossing restrictions, with specified exceptions such as a necessary passage around an obstruction under particular caution. A painted line is not an invitation to improvise an exception. [Road Signs Regulation, chapter 4, M1/M3/M8/M9][sign-law] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§9–11][traffic-law]

Supplementary panels qualify the sign above them: read the distance, length, direction, time or vehicle symbol as part of the instruction. A distance **to** a hazard and the **length** of a hazardous stretch answer different questions. [Supplementary panels][panels]

**Worked decision.** Your signal is green, but a queue fills the space beyond the junction. Wait where you will not obstruct crossing traffic rather than entering a space you cannot clear. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §20][traffic-law]

**Knowledge check.** Does amber mean accelerate to beat red? **Answer:** No. The rule is to stop; the exception concerns an unsafe stop after the change, not a wish to save time.

**Remember:** Read the whole instruction and the space beyond it.

### M03-L03 · Find your way without rushing

**Your goal:** Use direction, facility and service signs to prepare route choices early.

Direction information appears before and at decision points. An advance direction sign helps you understand a junction or exit before reaching it; lane direction information helps you select a suitable lane. Direction signs for walking and cycling serve those modes and should not be mistaken for a car route. Facility, service and tourism signs help locate destinations; they do not cancel restrictions on access. [Road Signs Regulation, chapter 2, F/G/H/I families][sign-law]

**Learning approach.** Know your next destination or route number, look for advance information and compare it with the road layout. Check the lane and movement required, then establish a safe opportunity to position the car. This is easier when navigation is prepared while parked and passengers understand that late instructions may be declined.

A symbol or route number may be easier to recognise than a place name. Learn the relevant symbols from the official catalogue rather than inventing an interpretation. Information signs can describe a changed route or other conditions; read enough of the message early to decide safely. [Transportstyrelsen: sign catalogue][signs]

**Requirement.** Destination signs can permit using the lane suited to your onward route, but changing into it still has to be safe. Do not cross a continuous lane boundary merely because you have chosen the wrong exit. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§7, 9 and 43][traffic-law]

**Worked decision.** A navigation instruction arrives after a driver has passed the safe lane-change opportunity. The driver continues in the permitted direction and finds another route. A missed turn is a planning problem; a sudden crossing of lanes creates a traffic problem.

**Knowledge check.** A service sign shows fuel at a junction. Does that guarantee that every approach or vehicle may enter the access road? **Answer:** No. Follow the access signs and restrictions as well as the destination information.

**Remember:** A route can be corrected later. An unsafe last-second manoeuvre cannot be planned away afterwards.

### M03-L04 · Leave room to respond

**Your goal:** Explain how observation, speed, reaction, braking, grip and following space affect your options.

**Requirements.** Choose a speed that preserves control and lets you stop within the road you can see and before a foreseeable obstruction. Your distance from the vehicle ahead must allow for it slowing or stopping. Weather, visibility, road condition, vehicle condition and loading all matter. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§2 and 14–16][traffic-law]

For an ordinary car, the general speed limits are 50 km/h within a built-up area and 70 km/h outside it, unless another applicable limit has been prescribed. The motorway default is 110 km/h; 120 km/h can be prescribed. Local and road-specific limits often differ, and a lower vehicle-combination limit still applies. Read the current signs and conditions rather than selecting a speed from the road’s appearance. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §17, chapter 4 §20 and chapter 9 §1][traffic-law]

Think of stopping in two parts. During the time between noticing a hazard and beginning effective braking, the vehicle still travels. After that, it travels while slowing. More speed consumes more distance during the same delay and increases the energy that must be removed. Less available friction reduces what the tyres can do for braking and steering. [Trafikverket: speed and impact][impact] · [VTI friction report, sections 4.2–4.3][friction]

**Illustrative calculation, not a stopping-distance prediction.** A speed of 50 km/h is approximately 13.9 metres per second: 50 ÷ 3.6. If a hypothetical response delay is one second, that is about 14 metres before braking begins. At 80 km/h the same assumed delay consumes about 22 metres. These figures are unit conversions using an assumed delay; people do not all react in one second, and braking distance has not yet been added.

**Learning approach.** Scan far enough ahead to see developing situations, while revisiting the near area, mirrors and places where someone may enter. Avoid staring at one hazard until everything else disappears from attention. Increase your margin when information is poor. A timed following-gap check can help you notice a short gap, but no fixed number is safe in every condition.

**Safety advice · Check a timed gap.** For the three-second following-distance guideline, select a fixed roadside point, such as a pole or sign. When the rear of the vehicle ahead passes it, start counting at a steady seconds pace. Stop when the front of your car reaches the same point. Arriving before three seconds indicates a short gap against that guideline: ease back smoothly and check again. Three seconds is a guideline, not a statutory fixed interval or a guarantee; poor grip, visibility or other hazards require more space and an appropriate speed. Keep observing the road while counting. [NSW Government: fixed-point following-gap method][following-gap]

**Fixed-point example.** The car ahead passes a sign. You count “one thousand and one, one thousand and two, one thousand and three”. If your car reaches the sign while you are still on “two”, increase the gap smoothly. At a hypothetical constant 60 km/h, three seconds represents 60 ÷ 3.6 × 3 = 50 metres. A timed gap therefore represents more metres at higher speed, without requiring you to estimate metres by eye.

**Safety advice · When someone follows too closely.** Increase your gap to the vehicle ahead gradually, allowing you to ease off and brake more gently if traffic slows. This gives the following driver more time to respond. Do not brake sharply to warn or punish them, or accelerate into an unsafe speed. If an emergency requires braking, brake as necessary; this advice does not mean withholding it. [Northern Ireland government: tailgater response][vehicle-faults]

**Tailgater example.** Maya has a close follower and sees brake lights farther ahead. She eases off early to create room in front and slows progressively. She cannot control the follower, but she can reduce the need for a sudden stop.

**Physical explanation · Twice the speed.** With the same effective grip and braking deceleration on the same gradient, braking distance is approximately proportional to speed squared: **2 × speed → 4 × braking distance**. In a constant-deceleration model, distance = speed² ÷ (2 × deceleration), with speed in metres per second. Doubling speed multiplies the numerator by four. Real conditions and brake response can change; this is a comparison under stated assumptions. [Trafikverket: VGU braking-distance equation][braking-model]

**Hypothetical comparison.** Assume a level road, unchanged vehicle/load/grip, constant braking deceleration of 5 m/s² and a one-second response delay. At 40 km/h, braking distance is about 12.3 m and reaction distance 11.1 m. At 80 km/h they are 49.4 m and 22.2 m: braking quadruples while reaction distance doubles. Total stopping distances are about 23.5 m and 71.6 m, so the total does **not** quadruple. These invented model conditions explain the relationship; they are not predicted stopping distances for your car.

**Approximate exercise model · Keep the methods separate.** Define v as speed in km/h and t as the supplied reaction time in seconds. Exact constant-speed reaction distance is (v ÷ 3.6) × t metres; braking is not included. When an exercise specifies the rough method below, use both rough estimates together rather than substituting an exact reaction value.

**Rough reaction estimate:** R = (v ÷ 10) × t × 3 metres. Divide speed by ten, multiply by the supplied seconds, then by three. [Trafiko: approximate stopping method][stopping-shortcut] documents the one-second shortcut; multiplication by a supplied t is editorial proportional arithmetic. Half a second gives half the one-second estimate; two seconds gives twice it. These are supplied exercise inputs, not claims about every driver's reaction time.

**Rough braking estimate:** B = (v ÷ 10)² × 0.4 metres. Divide speed by ten, square that result, then multiply by 0.4. [Trafiko: approximate stopping method][stopping-shortcut] describes this shortcut under good-brake, good-tyre and dry-asphalt assumptions. The coefficient is an exercise assumption, not a universal result for a real car or road. Supplied reaction time does not change the braking part in this model.

**Rough total:** S = R + B. Calculate both parts separately with metres, then add. Keep decimal precision during the working; round only the final result if a precision is requested. These shortcuts teach arithmetic, not a safe-distance guarantee or a prediction for an actual hazard.

**Source and assumption limits.** Trafiko is a commercial teaching source, cited only for these approximate shortcuts. [Trafikverket: stopping-distance principles][stopping-principles] supports reaction plus braking and dependence on speed and conditions, but does not supply these constants. Its illustrated distances have different assumptions and must not be mixed with this model. Real stopping depends on grip, gradient, tyres, brakes, response and other conditions. Retain the exact conversion and separate constant-deceleration comparison above under their own assumptions.

**Worked approximate examples · Calculate both parts.** Use the rough exercise equations consistently; each reaction time below is a supplied hypothetical input.

**Example A · 40 km/h and 0.5 seconds.** First v ÷ 10 = 4. Reaction R = 4 × 0.5 × 3 = 6 m. Braking B = 4² × 0.4 = 6.4 m. Total S = 6 + 6.4 = **12.4 m**. The half-second input affects reaction, not braking.

**Example B · 70 km/h and one second.** First v ÷ 10 = 7. Reaction R = 7 × 1 × 3 = 21 m. Braking B = 7² × 0.4 = 19.6 m. Total S = 21 + 19.6 = **40.6 m**. Squaring seven gives 49; do not multiply speed itself by 0.4.

**Example C · 50 km/h and two seconds.** First v ÷ 10 = 5. Reaction R = 5 × 2 × 3 = 30 m. Braking B = 5² × 0.4 = 10 m. Total S = 30 + 10 = **40 m**. Doubling reaction time doubles this model's reaction estimate, leaving braking unchanged.

**Compare separately with exact conversion.** At 50 km/h and two seconds, exact constant-speed reaction distance is (50 ÷ 3.6) × 2 = about **27.8 m**, compared with the shortcut's 30 m. Dividing the shortcut factor 0.3 by the exact conversion factor 1 ÷ 3.6 gives 1.08: an **8% overestimate** for reaction distance. Mixing 27.8 m with rough braking of 10 m gives about 37.8 m, not the exercise model's 40 m. Keep exact conversion separate from the rough-model answer.

**Try the approximate process.** Calculate reaction, braking and total for (A) 30 km/h and 0.5 seconds, (B) 80 km/h and one second, and (C) 100 km/h and two seconds. Give each part in metres, check the square, then add. Answers are in the separate lesson answer section.

**Worked decision.** You can see over the roof of the car ahead that traffic is slowing. Ease your approach early rather than waiting for its brake lights at a short distance. The benefit is having choices before braking becomes urgent.

**Knowledge check.** (1) If response delay doubles at the same speed, what happens to reaction distance? (2) How do you time a three-second following gap? (3) Why increase the forward gap when someone follows too closely? (4) With unchanged braking deceleration, how does doubled speed affect braking distance, and does total stopping distance also quadruple? (5) Complete the three approximate exercises above and explain why you should keep exact reaction conversion separate. **Answer:** (1) It doubles under the constant-speed assumption. (2) Count seconds from the rear of the car ahead passing a fixed point until your front reaches that point; three seconds is a guideline and conditions may require more. (3) More forward space allows gentler slowing and gives the follower more time; avoid unnecessary sudden braking. (4) Braking distance becomes approximately four times as long, but reaction distance doubles for the same response delay, so the combined total does not generally quadruple. (5) A: reaction 4.5 m, braking 3.6 m, total 8.1 m. B: reaction 24 m, braking 25.6 m, total 49.6 m. C: reaction 60 m, braking 40 m, total 100 m. Use both rough equations together; substituting exact reaction conversion changes the exercise-model answer.

**Remember:** Space is useful because it gives you time to notice, decide and act.

## 4. Negotiate shared space

A safe interaction needs both a rule and an understanding of how people are moving. Establish your duty first, then keep observing. Having priority never makes a collision acceptable.

### M04-L01 · Decide who goes first

**Your goal:** Explain junction decisions using priority instructions and right-hand priority, and recognise special situations.

**Requirements.** Where two vehicle paths cross, the general rule is to yield to a vehicle approaching from your right, except where another applicable rule or instruction determines the relationship. Road width, familiarity or the fact that you are travelling straight does not by itself establish priority. Right-hand priority can apply at a three-way junction or within a parking area. [Right-hand priority][priority] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §18][traffic-law]

Different duties apply when entering a road from premises, a car park, fuel station, similar roadside area, path or similar exit, or from terrain, a pedestrian street, walking-speed area or cycle street. After crossing a footway or cycle path to enter a road, the exit provision also matters. Do not decide solely from a kerb's appearance; identify the actual layout. Joining a priority road without an acceleration lane generally requires yielding, subject to the provision for joining without changing lane. Roundabout entry has its own duty. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§21–23][traffic-law]

To yield, show your intention by reducing speed or stopping in good time. Only continue when the other road user's position, distance and speed allow movement without danger or obstruction. A stop duty requires a complete stop even if the road appears empty. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§5 and 19][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Ask three questions: What controls this conflict? Whose path crosses mine? Can I make the decision early enough that the other person understands it? Do not wave someone into a situation you cannot assess for them.

**Worked decision.** You approach from a driveway on the right of a car travelling along the road. You yield under the exit rule. Being on the other driver's right does not displace that duty.

**Knowledge check.** You drive straight on a broad road and another car approaches from the right on a narrower ordinary road. No signs, signals or special rule apply. Who yields? **Answer:** You do. The width difference alone does not replace right-hand priority.

**Remember:** Determine the applicable rule before judging the available gap.

### M04-L02 · Turn with a complete picture

**Your goal:** Plan observation, signalling, position and margins for turns and lane changes.

**Requirements.** Before a turn, check that it will not obstruct oncoming traffic or traffic on the road you are entering. Pay attention to people travelling in the same direction, including those behind you. Position early: normally the right-hand lane for a right turn and the left-hand lane for a left turn where multiple approach lanes exist, subject to applicable signs, markings and space provisions. On a two-way road, a left turn is normally prepared near the road centre; on a one-way carriageway, near the left edge. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§24–29][traffic-law]

For a lane change or significant sideways movement, signal clearly and in time, then check that the movement can be made without danger or unnecessary obstruction. The indicator announces an intention, not a claim on a gap. Drivers who notice another vehicle intending to join their lane must adjust speed to help; when two lanes merge into one, both drivers must adapt with mutual consideration. These duties work together. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§43–44 and 65–66][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Read the path through the turn, not just the point at which you steer. Check the mirror view and the areas it does not cover. Look for a cyclist alongside before turning across their route. Recheck the receiving road, crossings and queue space. If the information changes, pause the manoeuvre rather than completing the original plan automatically.

**Safety advice · Wait with front wheels straight.** When stopped waiting to turn left across oncoming traffic, keep your front wheels pointing straight ahead. Your indicator communicates the intended turn; your wheels do not need to point along that intended route while you wait. If a vehicle hits you from behind, front wheels already turned left can direct the pushed car into oncoming traffic. Keeping them straight helps reduce that risk, but cannot guarantee protection in a collision. Begin steering into the turn only when there is a safe opportunity to make it. [New York DMV: waiting to turn left][left-turn-wheels]

**Waiting is different from turning.** Follow the positioning, signalling and observation requirements above. The wheel-position advice does not create permission to enter or block a junction, ignore a signal, or obstruct someone else's path. Continue observing oncoming traffic, the receiving road, crossings and vehicles behind you while waiting. Recheck the full path before moving.

**Practice example.** Sami is stopped in a suitable position, signalling left while oncoming cars pass. A passenger suggests turning the wheels left now to be ready. Sami keeps them straight: preparing the intention is different from starting the movement. Once the gap and receiving road permit a safe turn, he moves and steers into it. On rural roads, use M06-L01 to assess bends, crests and other risks before choosing a turning opportunity.

**Worked decision.** A driver signals to move right while a cyclist is beside the vehicle. The driver waits. Signalling earlier would have helped communication, but it would never have removed the need to ensure the cyclist's safety.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Does a driver in the continuing lane have no responsibility when someone is merging? (2) You are stopped waiting to turn left across oncoming traffic. Which way should the front wheels point, why, and when should you start steering into the turn? **Answer:** (1) No. They must adapt speed to facilitate an observed entry; the joining driver must still make a safe movement. (2) Keep the front wheels straight while waiting. Pre-turning them left can direct the car into oncoming traffic if it is struck from behind. Begin steering into the turn only when the movement can be made safely; a signal alone does not establish that opportunity.

**Remember:** Communicate the plan, then confirm the space is still there.

### M04-L03 · Crossings belong to people

**Your goal:** Distinguish pedestrian crossings, cycle passages and cycle crossings and apply the relevant duties.

**Requirements.** At an uncontrolled pedestrian crossing, yield to pedestrians who are on it or about to enter. Someone walking a bicycle is a pedestrian; someone riding one is not made a pedestrian merely by using the painted crossing. A person using a wheelchair at walking speed is covered by pedestrian rules. This distinction never removes the general duty to avoid danger. [Pedestrian crossings][pedestrians] · [Wheelchair users][wheelchairs] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 1 §4 and chapter 3 §61][traffic-law]

| Situation | Driver's duty |
|---|---|
| Uncontrolled cycle passage, approaching without turning | Adapt speed so cyclists or class II moped riders already on the passage are not endangered. A rider coming from a cycle path generally has a yielding duty. |
| Uncontrolled cycle passage after your turn or roundabout exit | Travel slowly and allow riders on or about to enter the passage an opportunity to pass. |
| Cycle crossing (*cykelöverfart*) | Yield to cyclists and class II moped riders on it or about to enter it. |

A cycle crossing has both markings and the cycle-crossing sign, together with a design that secures low vehicle speeds. Do not identify it only by painted blocks. [Transportstyrelsen: cycle passages and crossings][cycles] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§61–61a][traffic-law]

At a controlled pedestrian crossing, let people who entered correctly complete their passage even if your signal permits you to proceed. After turning across a controlled crossing, travel slowly and yield to pedestrians who have correctly entered or are about to enter. Corresponding duties apply to riders at controlled cycle passages. Keep the crossing clear when traffic ahead is queued. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§60–62][traffic-law]

**Worked decision.** You leave a roundabout and reach an uncontrolled cycle passage. A cyclist is about to enter. You slow and give the rider an opportunity to pass; applying only the straight-ahead passage rule would miss the significance of your exit.

**Knowledge check.** Is every cycle passage a cycle crossing with the same yielding duty? **Answer:** No. Identify the signs, controls and your own movement before applying the rule.

**Remember:** Expect a person to continue moving while you are interpreting the layout.

### M04-L04 · Choose a safe path through a roundabout

**Your goal:** Describe an approach, entry and exit plan that accounts for priority, lanes, signalling and nearby crossings.

**Requirements.** Travel anticlockwise and yield on entry to vehicles already circulating. Choose an appropriate lane for the onward route and obey signs and markings. There is no general requirement to signal solely for entering; signal lane changes and significant sideways movements, and signal right when leaving. Any lane change must be safe and must not create unnecessary obstruction. [Transportstyrelsen: roundabout guide][roundabout] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§13, 22, 43 and 65–66][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Read the layout before reaching the yield point. Reduce speed enough to judge circulating traffic and the available space. Observe crossings on the approach as separate conflicts. Inside, hold your intended path and continue checking nearby vehicles. A large vehicle may need more room than its apparent lane width suggests. [Roundabout guide][roundabout]

Plan the exit early. Moving from an inner lane across an outer lane is a lane change, even though you call the whole movement “leaving the roundabout.” Do not assume a vehicle beside you will leave at the same exit. If the exit cannot be taken safely, continue as the layout allows and re-plan. On leaving, check the crossing area again; the duties in M04-L03 still apply. [Roundabout guide][roundabout] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§60–62][traffic-law]

**Worked decision.** Your desired exit is approaching, but a car is alongside in the outer lane. You do not cut across it. You preserve a safe path and choose another opportunity consistent with the lane markings. Recovering the route is secondary to avoiding a conflict.

**Knowledge check.** Does a left signal give you permission to cross a neighbouring lane? **Answer:** No. Whatever the signal, a sideways movement requires a safe opportunity. Use the correct signal for the actual manoeuvre.

**Remember:** Entry, circulation, lane changes, exit and crossings are separate decisions within one journey.

### M04-R · Review and self-assessment

**Scenario:** Leave a car park, turn left, pass a cycle crossing and enter a two-lane roundabout.

State each duty before choosing a gap. **Review guide:** Apply the exit rule, check oncoming and receiving-road traffic, identify the crossing correctly, yield on roundabout entry and plan any lane change and exit without assuming priority.

Which conflict would be hardest to observe while also controlling the car? Record the consequence of noticing it late and arrange targeted supervised practice. [Syllabus self-assessment, chapter 3 §3][syllabus]

## 5. Make room in busy streets

In a busy street, a small change can create a new conflict: a door opens, someone steps around a parked van, or a bus prepares to leave. Good driving leaves room for those changes.

### M05-L01 · See the person behind the movement

**Your goal:** Anticipate the needs of different road users without assuming that everyone sees, hears or moves as you do.

**Requirements.** Show particular consideration for children, older people, school patrols and people whose disability or illness is apparent and affects their use of traffic. Reduce speed appropriately near children, stopped passenger transport and other situations with foreseeable risks. Give pedestrians sufficient room and time when meeting or passing them. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 2 §1 and chapter 3 §§15 and 58][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Look for the space a person needs, not only the space they occupy now. A child may be hidden by a parked car. A cyclist may need to move out from an obstruction. A wheelchair user may need a usable kerb transition. You cannot reliably infer someone's hearing, vision or intentions from a quick glance. Allow time and observe their movement rather than demanding a response through the horn.

Horses and riders also belong in your traffic assessment. Vehicle rules apply to riding and leading horses in relevant respects; they are not simply pedestrians because they travel slowly. Give them an unhurried, spacious interaction, and avoid unnecessary noise. This is a cautious application of the general duty of care, not a fixed passing-distance rule. [Transportstyrelsen: horses in traffic][horses]

**Bus rule.** Where the maximum permitted speed is 50 km/h or lower, a driver approaching a bus whose driver signals to leave a bus stop must slow and give it the opportunity to leave, stopping if needed. The bus driver must also take particular care. If passing on the right of a bus or tram stopped at a stop without a refuge, stop to allow boarding or alighting passengers free passage. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§45 and 58][traffic-law]

**Bus comparison · Check the road limit.** The threshold concerns the maximum permitted speed on the road, not how fast you happen to be travelling. At a 70 km/h limit, the special bus-stop departure rule above does not apply. The bus must wait for an entry that can be made without danger or unnecessary obstruction. You still have a duty to adapt speed to facilitate an observed entry into your lane. A bus indicator does not create unconditional priority, and the higher road limit does not excuse deliberately blocking it. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§43–45][traffic-law]

| Road limit | Duties when the bus signals to leave its stop |
|---|---|
| 50 km/h or lower | Slow and let the bus leave; stop if needed. The bus driver must take particular care. |
| 70 km/h, ordinary entry into your lane | The special bus-exit duty does not apply. The bus waits for a safe entry; you adapt speed to facilitate the observed entry. |

**Safety advice · Let a guide dog work.** Do not pet, call to or talk to a working guide dog to attract its attention. Distraction can endanger the person it is helping. Allow the pair room and time; if you need to communicate, address the handler rather than trying to direct the dog. The handler chooses the route and remains in control. For example, a passenger beside your parked car wants to greet a guide dog at a crossing. Ask them to leave it working and keep the path clear. [MFD: meeting a working guide dog][guide-dog]

**Traffic terminology · A bicycle is a vehicle.** A person riding an ordinary pedal bicycle is operating a vehicle, even without an engine. “Vehicle” does not mean only “motor vehicle”. Apply the relevant cyclist duties and protections; crossing distinctions remain in M04-L03. [Transportstyrelsen: bicycle definition][bicycle-definition]

**Worked decision.** On a 40 km/h street, a bus signals out. You ease speed early and leave room rather than trying to squeeze past. You also watch for people near the stop; making space for the bus is not your only task. Compare an otherwise ordinary 70 km/h road: the bus waits for a safe entry, while you still adapt speed to help an observed entry into your lane. Travelling at 40 on that 70 road does not activate the special bus rule.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Does a narrow visible gap beside a cyclist prove passing is safe? (2) A bus signals from its stop on a 70 km/h road while you drive at 40. Does the special bus rule apply, and what must both drivers do? (3) Should you greet a working guide dog? (4) Is a person riding a pedal bicycle operating a vehicle? **Answer:** (1) No. Consider possible rider movement, obstacles, oncoming traffic and a safe lateral margin; wait if needed. (2) No: the road limit determines the threshold. The bus must enter safely without unnecessary obstruction; you must adapt speed to facilitate the observed entry into your lane. At limits of 50 or lower, slow and let the bus leave, stopping if necessary; the bus driver must take particular care. (3) No. Avoid distracting it by petting, calling or talking to it; communicate with the handler if needed. (4) Yes. A bicycle is a vehicle even without an engine.

**Remember:** Predict possible needs without assuming a person will behave exactly as expected.

### M05-L02 · When the street changes purpose

**Your goal:** Distinguish ordinary streets from pedestrian streets, walking-speed areas and cycle streets.

**Requirements.** On a pedestrian street (*gågata*) and in a walking-speed area (*gångfartsområde*), drive no faster than walking pace and yield to pedestrians. The general rule prohibits parking; a specifically signed lawful parking arrangement must be assessed on its own terms. Walking pace is a condition to match, not a universal speedometer number. [Special streets][special-streets] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 8 §1 and chapter 10 §1][traffic-law]

Motor vehicles may use a pedestrian street only to cross it, apart from the specified necessary journeys: goods to or from shops or equivalent premises; goods or residents to or from addresses there; guests to or from hotels or equivalent premises; and sick people or people with mobility impairments to or from an address. A convenient shortcut does not qualify. Check additional signs and local restrictions. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 8 §1][traffic-law]

On a cycle street (*cykelgata*), the maximum is 30 km/h, and motor vehicles must adapt speed to bicycle traffic. Vehicles entering from another road yield to vehicles already on it. Parking is restricted to specially provided places. When leaving a cycle street for another road, the exit yielding rule applies. [Special streets][special-streets] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §21 and chapter 8 §1a][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** A change in street function changes your expectations. People may use more of the width; a car's arrival should not force them into hurried avoidance. Read the beginning and ending signs and identify whether your proposed journey is permitted before entering.

**Worked decision.** Your navigation suggests a pedestrian street as a shortcut. You continue to another permitted route. The navigation suggestion does not establish an exemption from the access rule.

**Knowledge check.** Is 30 km/h always appropriate on a cycle street? **Answer:** No. It is a maximum. You must adapt to the actual bicycle traffic and any lower speed needed for safety.

**Remember:** Recognise the area's purpose and adapt before crossing its boundary.

### M05-L03 · Choose a lawful place to stop

**Your goal:** Distinguish stopping from parking and interpret the rules, signs and time panels for a proposed place.

Parking is leaving a vehicle stationary for a reason other than traffic conditions, avoiding danger, boarding or alighting, or loading or unloading goods. Remaining behind the wheel does not automatically make waiting “stopping.” Nor does switching on hazard lights create permission. [Transportstyrelsen: stopping and parking][parking]

**Core requirements.** Do not stop or park where you create danger, unnecessarily obstruct traffic or hide a sign or signal. Normally use the right side in your direction of travel; one-way roads and specified rail-track situations have exceptions. Secure the vehicle against rolling and prevent unauthorised use. Open doors only without danger or unnecessary inconvenience. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§47, 50 and 52][traffic-law]

| Place | General restriction for an ordinary car |
|---|---|
| Pedestrian crossing, cycle passage or cycle crossing | No stopping on it or within 10 m before it. |
| Road junction | No stopping in it or within 10 m of the nearest edge of the crossing carriageway. |
| Crossing footway or cycle path | No stopping on it or within 10 m before it. |
| Tunnel, road underpass, hidden crest or obscured bend | No stopping in the prohibited location. |
| Beside a continuous lane boundary | No stopping if less than 3 m remains, unless a broken line lies between the car and the continuous line. |
| Railway level crossing | No stopping on it; no parking within 30 m on the road. |
| Property access | No parking in front of it or where vehicle access is substantially obstructed. |
| Priority road or signed passing place | No parking under the general rule; check any lawful signed provision. |

Other prohibitions cover cycle lanes, hatched areas, roundabouts and lanes reserved for regular public transport. These examples are not an exhaustive catalogue of local restrictions. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§47–55][traffic-law]

At a marked bus stop, other vehicles may stop only for boarding or alighting without obstructing the relevant service. If its extent is not marked, the restricted area is normally 20 m before and 5 m after the stop sign. Loading goods is not the same exception as letting a passenger get out. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §54][traffic-law]

**Read the whole parking instruction.** On a T6 time panel, ordinary black/white times without brackets mean weekdays other than the day before a Sunday or public holiday; bracketed times mean those preceding days; red times mean Sundays and public holidays. A named day applies to that day. Check the actual date, fee, duration, vehicle group and any permit condition. A disabled-parking permit has defined benefits, not unrestricted permission to stop anywhere. [Road Signs Regulation, T6–T8 and T16–T19][sign-law] · [Parking permits][parking-permit]

On municipally maintained public places within built-up areas and within public-road areas, parking is normally limited to 24 consecutive hours on weekdays other than the day before a Sunday or public holiday. This is not a blanket rule for every private car park. Where a parking disc is required, set it to the next half-hour after arrival; if you arrive before the restricted period and remain into it, set its beginning time. Display it so it can be read. Always check the applicable signs and conditions. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §49a][traffic-law]

**Worked decision.** It is an ordinary Tuesday, not a holiday or the day before one. A parking sign permits at most two hours during black unbracketed 09–18. You arrive at 10:00. The two-hour restriction applies; check the other conditions before leaving the car.

**Knowledge check.** Can you wait 8 m before a pedestrian crossing with the engine running? **Answer:** No. The general prohibition includes stopping, not just parking.

**Remember:** First check whether the place is lawful at all; then check its conditions.

### M05-L04 · Manoeuvre where people may appear

**Your goal:** Plan low-speed parking and reversing while observing changing space.

**Requirements.** Reverse or turn around only without danger or obstruction to other road users. Secure the parked vehicle and check before opening a door or letting passengers out. Parking inside a marked space requires the wheels to remain within its boundary. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§46, 49–50][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Select a place that offers both a safe entry and a safe departure. Check the full route of the vehicle: front corners can move sideways while you watch behind. Revisit both sides and the path ahead as the manoeuvre develops. Do not treat a camera, sensor or a helper's signal as proof that every part of the area is clear.

Stop when information is lost. If you need to inspect the area, first secure the vehicle and choose a safe way to do so. In supervised practice, learn the car's dimensions and the effect of steering gradually. A useful successful outcome is not “I parked in one movement”; it is “I kept control and did not move into uncertain space.”

**Worked decision.** A delivery vehicle blocks the view of the footway behind your parking place. Rather than reversing across it in one continuous movement, you reassess the layout with your supervisor and choose a method or another place that provides the necessary view. If a pedestrian enters the path, you wait.

**Knowledge check.** Is a pedestrian obliged to avoid your car because you began reversing first? **Answer:** No. Your reversing duty remains; stop before creating a conflict.

**Remember:** A small adjustment or a pause can be evidence of good judgement.

### M05-R · Review and self-assessment

**Scenario:** You reach a busy neighbourhood to collect a passenger. A bus is signalling out, a cyclist is beside parked cars and the nearest space is before a crossing.

Explain how you will approach and choose a lawful collection point. **Review guide:** Account for the bus duty where applicable, the cyclist's space, the crossing restriction and safe boarding. Convenience does not create a parking exemption.

Record one observation that was difficult to make early, and plan a supervised exercise to improve it. [Syllabus self-assessment, chapter 3 §3][syllabus]

## 6. Travel beyond the town

Higher speeds reduce the time available between seeing a problem and reaching it. On rural roads and faster routes, plan further ahead and keep enough room to change your mind.

### M06-L01 · Read the rural road

**Your goal:** Identify risks from bends, crests, narrow roads, animals and slow vehicles and justify speed and position changes.

**Requirements.** Keep right when meeting another vehicle and leave a safe lateral gap. If an obstruction on your side prevents a safe meeting, stop if necessary to let the approaching vehicle pass. Your speed must suit the visible road and foreseeable hazards, with particular care at bends, crests and narrow meetings. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§14–15 and 30][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Read the road in sections: the visible straight, the entry to a bend, the portion hidden behind a crest and the next place someone might join. Never extend the clear section in your imagination. A vehicle emerging from a field or a cyclist beyond a bend changes what seemed to be an empty road.

Wild animals and reindeer can appear throughout the year. Trafikverket highlights dawn and dusk and periods of increased animal movement, but the absence of those conditions is not a guarantee. A wildlife fence can be interrupted by access points and does not remove the need to observe. [Trafikverket: wildlife][wildlife]

A slow vehicle's appearance may resemble a car even when its permitted speed and construction differ. Trafikverket advises other drivers to keep distance and pass only where appropriate. Stay far enough back to see around it and evaluate the road, rather than moving close to demand that it hurry. [Slow vehicles and other traffic][slow-vehicles]

**Turn planning · Choose a safer approach.** On an ordinary two-way rural road, heavy traffic may make a direct left turn unsafe. Continue to a suitable, permitted place or route where you can change direction safely, then return to approach the destination with a right turn if that movement is allowed and safe. This can avoid hazardous waiting and crossing the opposing stream at the original entrance. It is a planning alternative, not permission to turn around immediately on the carriageway. If no safe legal return route exists, choose another route or postpone. [Trafikverket: example of routing to a turning place][rural-turn-alternative]

**Legal boundary.** Before turning or leaving the road, protect oncoming traffic and traffic on the road you enter; take particular care of following traffic. A turnaround must cause neither danger nor obstruction. Signs and access restrictions still apply. Never turn around or reverse on a motorway or expressway. If you can wait safely and legally for a left turn, remember the advice in M04-L02 to keep the front wheels straight; that advice does not make an unsafe turn suitable. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§24, 26, 29 and 46; chapter 9 §§1–2][traffic-law]

**Worked rural alternative.** A farm entrance is on your left and busy traffic leaves no safe opportunity. You continue to an accessible junction route that legally and safely brings you back in the opposite direction. The entrance is then on your right. Check people, traffic and the entrance again before turning; the revised route does not guarantee a safe final movement.

**Wildlife observation · Time and terrain.** Give extra attention at dawn and dusk, near animal habitats and where fences end or have openings. Scan woodland edges, bushes and small waterways beside the road: Trafikverket's road-project study describes these as animal routes that can conceal an approach. Its national guidance also describes animal passages through fence openings. These are cues for observation and speed planning, not predictions that animals only cross there. [Trafikverket: wildlife and fence passages][wildlife] · [Trafikverket: habitat example, page 9][wildlife-habitat]

**Apply the cues.** At dusk, woodland meets a field beside a gap in the fence. Read the edges and opening as possible animal approaches, and choose speed for the view and foreseeable hazard. Keep observing even on fenced stretches. Prevention belongs here; actions after a collision are in M10-L01.

**Worked decision.** You approach a narrow bridge with a van coming towards you and an obstruction on your side. You reduce speed before the narrowing and stop if necessary. Do not move into the van's path simply because you reached the obstruction first.

**Knowledge check.** (1) No vehicle is visible beyond a crest. Is the road proved clear? (2) Heavy traffic makes your rural left turn unsafe. What alternative can you plan? (3) Which time and roadside cues call for extra wildlife attention? **Answer:** (1) No. The view is limited; choose speed and position for that uncertainty. (2) Continue to a suitable permitted turning place or route, change direction safely and return for a right turn if allowed and safe. If no such route exists, choose another route or postpone. Do not make an immediate unsafe turnaround. (3) Dawn/dusk, animal habitats such as woodland edges and bushes, and fence endings/openings. Animals can also appear elsewhere and at other times.

**Remember:** Let what you can actually see determine the available space.

### M06-L02 · Is passing worth the risk?

**Your goal:** Compare waiting with overtaking using visibility, speed difference, space and a safe completion path.

**Requirements.** Overtake only without danger and with sufficient clear road. Do not start when a following driver has begun overtaking, or pass on the left when the vehicle ahead signals an intended leftward movement. Leave safe lateral clearance and a safe return opportunity. When being overtaken, do not increase speed or otherwise make the pass harder. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§31–38][traffic-law]

The normal passing side is left. Exceptions include a vehicle turning or clearly preparing to turn left, and the specified rules for marked same-direction lanes on roads limited to 70 km/h or less. Passing in queues or destination lanes has separate provisions; it does not remove the need to protect people at crossings. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§7, 31 and 41–42][traffic-law]

**Restrictions to recognise.** Overtaking is generally prohibited immediately before or in a road junction, with specified exceptions for right-side overtaking, police/signal control, or an established yielding/stopping duty on the crossing road. Similar restrictions apply at level crossings, with exceptions for barriers or road-junction-type traffic signals. At an obscured bend or crest, do not use an opposing lane to overtake; the detailed provision contains limited exceptions. Section 39 also contains an exception for passing two-wheeled vehicles without sidecars. That exception does not override the danger prohibition, continuous-line restrictions or the uncontrolled-crossing prohibition. None of these exceptions proves a manoeuvre safe. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §39][traffic-law]

Do not overtake immediately before or on an uncontrolled pedestrian crossing, uncontrolled cycle passage or cycle crossing. Where parallel-lane passing is legally not classified as overtaking, the special crossing rule still requires a speed allowing an immediate stop for a person on the crossing or passage. Obey signs and continuous-line restrictions as well. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§11, 40–41][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Before moving, identify the full pass and return path, not merely the first gap. If completing it depends on another driver braking or on exceeding the limit, decline it. Small apparent time savings are not a reason to consume every margin. [Trafikverket: speed and journey time][time]

**Worked decision.** A vehicle ahead travels slightly slower than you want. An approaching bend hides the final part of the pass. You stay behind at a useful distance. The first few clear metres do not establish a complete overtaking opportunity.

**Knowledge check.** If the vehicle ahead accelerates during your pass, must you complete it at any cost? **Answer:** No. Reassess and take the safest available action; do not treat finishing as more important than avoiding a collision. Plan with enough margin that abandoning is possible before committing.

**Remember:** Choosing not to pass is a complete decision.

### M06-L03 · Join faster traffic calmly

**Your goal:** Plan motorway entry, lane choice, separation and exit, including a missed exit.

**Requirements.** Motorways are for eligible motor vehicles and combinations designed and permitted to travel at least 40 km/h; class I mopeds are excluded. This is an eligibility condition, not a command to maintain 40 km/h in a queue. Enter and leave through the designated starts, ends and access routes. Do not reverse, turn around, use the central divider or cross-connections, or stop outside designated parking/rest areas except as an unavoidable incident requires. The relevant restrictions also apply to expressways (*motortrafikled*) and their access routes. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 9 §§1–2][traffic-law]

In an acceleration lane, adapt speed to the traffic in the lane you intend to join and enter as soon as it can be done without danger or unnecessary obstruction. A safe joining movement is your responsibility; other drivers who notice the intended entry must adapt speed to facilitate it. Entry without an acceleration lane has a yielding rule. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§21, 23 and 43][traffic-law]

Normally use the rightmost lane intended for your vehicle. Keep a following distance suited to speed, visibility and conditions; do not copy a car ahead that is following too closely. Signs may establish a lower maximum than a road's general limit, and your vehicle combination may have a lower limit again. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§2 and 7, chapter 4 §20 and chapter 9 §1][traffic-law]

For an exit, take the suitable lane in good time and enter the deceleration lane as soon as possible. If you miss the exit, continue to a permitted exit and re-plan; never reverse to recover it. A breakdown is dealt with in M10-L01, not by using the shoulder as a convenient place to check navigation.

**After the motorway · Recheck your speed.** After a long period at a steady motorway speed, your perception can adapt to that pace. On a slower road, the correct lower speed may feel unusually slow. This effect is often called speed adaptation or velocitation. Check the speedometer regularly to judge actual speed and adjust to the current limit and conditions. The feeling alone is not evidence that the speedometer is faulty. [ODOT: speed perception and speedometer checks][speed-adaptation]

**Worked transition.** After a long motorway trip, you leave onto a road signed 50 km/h. At 50, the car feels as if it is crawling. Confirm the speedometer reading, read the new road and retain a speed suited to its limit, visibility and hazards. Do not accelerate merely to recover the motorway's familiar feeling.

**Worked decision.** Your exit sign appears while another vehicle occupies the lane you would need to cross. You do not force the move. You continue safely and tell your passenger the route will change.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Does a posted motorway limit override a lower trailer-combination limit? (2) After prolonged motorway driving, the lower speed on your new road feels too slow. What explains that feeling, and what should guide your speed? **Answer:** (1) No. Use the lower applicable road/vehicle limit and reduce further for conditions. (2) Perception may have adapted to the motorway pace. Check the speedometer and current limit, then adapt to visibility and hazards. Feeling slow alone does not prove a faulty instrument or justify accelerating.

**Remember:** Prepare the next movement before the road makes it urgent.

### M06-L04 · Expect an interrupted route

**Your goal:** Interpret railway-crossing and roadworks information and approach changed lanes, queues and barriers safely.

**Railway requirements.** Approach with particular care and at a speed that allows a stop before the crossing. Do not enter when a train or tram is approaching, a red signal shows, an audible warning sounds, a barrier is lowering, lowered or rising, or you might have to stop on the tracks. Cross without unnecessary delay only when it is permitted and there is room beyond. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 2 §7][traffic-law]

**Two different emergencies.** Trafikverket says that if warnings begin while you are already crossing, continue out. If a functioning vehicle is trapped between the barriers, drive through the barrier to leave the tracks. If the vehicle has broken down on the tracks, evacuate everyone promptly, move well away and call 112. Give the crossing ID from the crossbuck sign if available without returning to danger. Do not stay on the tracks attempting a repair or pushing exercise. [Trafikverket: level-crossing safety][rail]

**Roadworks.** Expect changed lanes, reduced speeds, workers and queues. Follow the current signs, signals and authorised guard directions; allow room around work areas and keep distance. A familiar road may now have a different usable path. Temporary guidance deserves attention before you reach the first barrier, not after you have entered the work area. [Trafikverket: roadworks][works] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 2 §2 and chapter 3 §15][traffic-law]

**Worked decision.** Traffic beyond a railway crossing stops while the barriers are still raised. You wait before the crossing. An open barrier is not proof that there is space to get clear. Later, at roadworks, you read the temporary lane instructions instead of following the old route from memory.

**Knowledge check.** Your engine fails while the car is on the tracks. Is the priority to protect the car or to evacuate and call for help? **Answer:** Evacuate people, get away from the railway and call 112. A disabled vehicle and a vehicle still able to drive through a barrier require different responses.

**Remember:** Never enter a space unless you can clear it, and protect people first when the situation changes.

### M06-R · Review and self-assessment

**Scenario:** Your rural trip involves a slow vehicle, a motorway entry, temporary roadworks and a railway crossing with a queue beyond it.

Identify where you might have to wait or change the route. **Review guide:** A complete overtaking path, a safe entry gap, current temporary instructions and clear space beyond the tracks matter more than maintaining progress.

Record one situation in which impatience could change your judgement. Explain the risk and identify a practice or planning action that preserves your margin. [Syllabus self-assessment, chapter 3 §3][syllabus]

## 7. Adapt when conditions change

A journey can become more demanding without any single dramatic event. Darkness, reduced grip, distraction and tiredness can combine. Reassess while you still have comfortable choices.

### M07-L01 · Drive within what you can see

**Your goal:** Choose a visibility and lighting strategy for darkness, fog and glare.

**Requirements.** Your speed must allow a stop within the visible road and before foreseeable hazards. Darkness does not create a separate allowance to travel beyond the distance you can assess. Keep the required lamps on; daytime running lights alone may be insufficient when conditions deteriorate. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§14–15 and 68–71][traffic-law]

Use main beam when needed for a sufficient field of view, but not where the road is adequately lit, where it can dazzle an approaching driver or an adjacent rail/ship operator, or at close distance behind another vehicle. Dipped beam is the usual driving light. In fog or heavy precipitation, front fog lights may replace dipped beam; the prohibited combinations must not be used together. A rear fog light is restricted to fog, heavy precipitation or comparable reduced visibility and must not dazzle a following driver. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§69–72][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Make lighting and speed one decision. If you dip the lights and can see less far, reassess speed immediately. Check the edges of the visible road for people and animals rather than assuming a dark area is empty. Keep windows and mirrors clear enough for a useful view and learn demisting controls before a difficult journey.

Automatic lighting is an aid, not confirmation that every required lamp is on. In a car whose daytime-light mode leaves the rear lights off, check the actual setting when darkness or bad weather arrives. [Transportstyrelsen: vehicle lighting][lights]

**Lighting choice · Name the prohibited pair.** Do not use dipped headlights and front fog lights together. In fog or heavy precipitation, front fog lights may replace dipped headlights; they are not an extra light to add to them. Select a permitted mode and check it on the actual vehicle. [Transportstyrelsen: dipped beam and front fog lights][lights-combinations]

**Central vision · Read the detail.** Central vision supplies the fine detail where you are looking directly. Reading the precise wording on a road sign relies particularly on it. Merely noticing a sign at the edge of your view does not establish what its text says. [Official driver-vision guidance][vision-roles]

**Peripheral vision · Notice, then inspect.** Peripheral or side vision surrounds your direct focus. It can alert you to movement, a developing hazard or a traffic change outside that focus. Move your eyes, and head where needed, to inspect the relevant object directly while maintaining control and awareness of the road. For example, notice movement near the verge, then look to identify whether it is a person about to enter your path. Keep scanning; staring at one sign can leave other information unattended. [Official driver-vision guidance][vision-roles]

**One light · Keep width uncertain.** One approaching light at night does not prove a narrow motorcycle. A wider car can have a failed headlight. Transportstyrelsen documents defective car headlights; the practical inference is to allow for a vehicle whose full width is not yet visible. Preserve a safe lateral margin and adapt speed to the view rather than squeezing past on a guessed width. [Transportstyrelsen: defective car headlights][lamp-defects]

**Worked decision.** Rain becomes heavy during a daytime trip. The dashboard remains illuminated, but the driver checks and selects the appropriate exterior lighting rather than inferring that the rear lights must be on. The driver also reduces speed for the shorter usable view.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Can you maintain speed solely because the limit is unchanged after dipping main beam? (2) May dipped headlights and front fog lights be used together? (3) Which vision role reads precise sign text, and which can alert you to movement outside direct focus? (4) Does one approaching light prove a narrow motorcycle? **Answer:** (1) No; reassess safe speed for the view. (2) No. Front fog lights may replace dipped beam in fog/heavy precipitation. (3) Central vision reads fine detail; peripheral vision can detect movement/hazards. Look directly to inspect. (4) No. It may be a wider car with a failed lamp; preserve clearance.

**Remember:** Adjust speed to the information the light actually gives you.

### M07-L02 · Grip is a changing resource

**Your goal:** Explain how surface conditions, tyres and driver inputs affect stability, steering and braking.

Tyres need friction to change the vehicle's speed and direction. Braking while cornering shares that capacity; ABS can help preserve steering forces during braking, but the available friction still limits the result. Water, snow and ice change the conditions at the tyre contact, so dry-road expectations cannot simply be carried into them. [VTI friction report, sections 4.2–4.3][friction]

**Requirements for ordinary light cars and their trailers, checked 1 October 2026.** Between 1 December and 31 March, winter tyres or equivalent equipment are required when winter conditions exist. Snow, ice, slush or frost on any part of the road counts; police determine conditions at the location. The minimum tread depth for the ordinary light-vehicle case is 3 mm. Studless winter tyres for the car need the mountain/snowflake marking. [Winter tyres][winter]

Studded tyres are permitted from 1 October to 15 April and outside that period when winter conditions exist or are expected. Do not mix studded and non-studded tyres on an ordinary passenger car or light goods vehicle. In winter conditions, a studded towing car requires a studded trailer; the reverse combination is allowed. A light-trailer transition permits specified winter-designed M+S tyres through 30 November 2028. Exemptions and heavier-vehicle cases need their own checks; they are not the ordinary car rule. [Winter tyres][winter] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 4 §§18a–18d][traffic-law]

For summer conditions, the minimum tread depth is 1.6 mm. Transportstyrelsen recommends placing the tyres with the best grip or greatest tread depth at the rear and checking pressure against the vehicle's instructions. A legal minimum is not a promise of equal performance in every condition. [Transportstyrelsen: tyres][tyre-basics]

**Learning approach.** Reduce speed and increase space before reaching uncertain grip. Avoid demanding abrupt braking, steering and acceleration together. Do not experiment with skid recovery in ordinary traffic. Establish the actual vehicle's emergency-braking behaviour through appropriate instruction and risk training. [Trafikverket: winter driving advice][tyre-advice] · [Risk training][risk]

**Surface change · A bridge can freeze first.** A bridge is exposed to cold air above and below. That exposure can cool it faster than neighbouring road supported by ground, so moisture can freeze on the bridge while the approach still appears clear. It is a possibility, not a rule that every bridge always freezes first. Anticipate uncertain grip and reduce speed before reaching the bridge. [National Weather Service: bridge cooling][bridge-cooling]

**Rain on a cold road · Ice can hide.** Rainwater can freeze when it meets a road surface cold enough for ice to form, producing a thin, nearly invisible slippery layer. The road may look wet, and the air temperature can be above zero while the surface is still below freezing. Near-zero air alone does not prove ice, but it calls for attention to surface conditions, more space and lower speed. [Trafikverket: deceptive ice and cold roads][ice-risk]

**First rain after dry weather · Residues matter.** During a dry spell, dust, dirt and oily residues can accumulate. The first rain can mix with these contaminants and make the road unusually slippery. Reassess speed and separation as rain starts; do not assume a light shower immediately washes the surface clean or that a fixed time restores grip. [County Fire: rain after a dry spell][first-rain]

**Worked surface decision.** A light shower begins after dry weather as you approach a bridge in near-freezing conditions. Plan for both wet residues and possible local ice. The clear-looking approach does not measure the grip on the bridge.

**Rear stability · Why the best grip belongs at the rear.** Transportstyrelsen recommends the best-grip or deepest-tread tyres at the rear to reduce skid risk in braking and bends, for both front- and rear-wheel-drive cars. Better rear grip helps reduce the risk of the rear sliding outward and rotating the car. Suitable tyres and stability assistance still cannot guarantee control. [Transportstyrelsen: best tyres at the rear][tyre-basics] · [VTI: rear grip and oversteer][handling]

**Oversteer · Rear grip loss.** In the loss-of-grip situation, the rear tyres lose sufficient lateral grip, the tail swings outward and the car rotates more than intended into the turn. This can develop into a spin. [VTI: oversteer and rear stability][handling]

**Understeer · Front grip loss.** In the loss-of-grip situation, the front tyres cannot supply the lateral grip needed for the requested turn. The car turns less than intended and follows a straighter, wider path; adding more steering does not guarantee the requested curve. These are grip-loss explanations, not a full definition of every vehicle's normal handling characteristics. [VTI: handling paths][handling] · [NHTSA: front-axle grip limit][front-grip]

**Aquaplaning · Water interrupts contact.** If water builds between tyres and road, it can support the tyre on a film and reduce or remove effective tyre-road contact. Steering and braking then have less grip to work with. Reduce speed before standing water and uncertain wet surfaces. [PennDOT: aquaplaning][wet-driving]

**Response · Ease off smoothly.** If aquaplaning starts and there is space to slow gradually, ease off the accelerator, keep steering steady and avoid abrupt steering or hard braking. Make only gentle necessary corrections as contact returns. This is different from M02-L01's ABS emergency-stop instruction: ABS cannot create grip where a tyre is separated from the road by water. An immediate collision emergency needs action appropriate to the actual situation; this advice does not establish a universal ban on braking. [PennDOT: aquaplaning response][wet-driving]

**Worked grip comparison.** A rear slide swinging the tail outward is oversteer; a front slide that prevents the intended curve is understeer. A light, unresponsive steering feel over standing water raises a different possibility: aquaplaning. Recognise the mechanism, preserve margins and practise vehicle-specific emergency handling only with suitable instruction.

**Worked decision.** Your car has suitable winter tyres, but the next bend is partly shaded and looks different from the road behind. You reduce speed before the bend and preserve margin. Equipment does not establish the grip at that particular point.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Does legal tread depth guarantee dry-road spacing on ice? (2) Why can a bridge freeze first? (3) What can rain form on a sufficiently cold road? (4) Why can first rain after dry weather be unusually slippery? (5) Why place the best-grip tyres at the rear? (6) Compare oversteer and understeer in a grip-loss situation. (7) What is aquaplaning, and how should you respond when there is room to slow gradually? **Answer:** (1) No; adapt speed and gap. (2) Cold air cools it above and below. (3) A nearly invisible slippery ice layer. (4) Water mixes with accumulated dirt/oily residues. (5) To reduce rear grip loss, tail swing and rotation. (6) Oversteer: rear grip loss, tail outward, more rotation than intended. Understeer: front grip loss, less turn and a wider/straighter path. (7) A water film reduces effective tyre-road contact. Ease off the accelerator, keep steering steady and avoid abrupt steering/hard braking; use gentle necessary corrections.

**Remember:** Tyres and assistance systems support a cautious plan; they do not create unlimited grip.

### M07-L03 · Protect your attention

**Your goal:** Recognise distraction, stress and fatigue and choose an interruption or alternative travel plan.

**Requirements.** During road travel, a driver may use communication equipment only if it does not adversely affect driving. Holding the equipment in the hand while using it is prohibited. Hands-free use is therefore not automatically acceptable: the effect on driving still matters. You must also not drive when exhaustion or another condition prevents safe control. [Transportstyrelsen: communication equipment][phone] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §1 and chapter 4 §10e][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Prepare navigation before setting off, place the phone away from casual handling and agree when passengers can help. If a conversation, screen task or emotional reaction is consuming the attention needed for the road, end it. A safe place to stop is the place to resolve a complicated route or urgent message.

**Attention link.** Use M07-L01's notice-then-inspect distinction when scanning. A conversation that consumes attention can delay recognising what peripheral vision has alerted you to.

Treat fatigue as a reason to change the journey. Plan rest opportunities and stop safely when concentration cannot be maintained. Do not wait for a dramatic event to prove that you are too tired. Trafikverket recommends setting out rested, allowing time and taking breaks on longer journeys. No universal break interval in this material certifies that you can continue safely. [Journey advice][planning]

**Worked decision.** A driver misses a sign during an intense hands-free conversation. The useful response is not to argue that the device is legal to use. End the conversation, reassess fitness and stop safely if attention cannot be restored.

**Knowledge check.** Is entering a destination on a mounted phone necessarily allowed because it is not hand-held? **Answer:** No. Equipment use must not adversely affect driving. Prepare it while parked or stop somewhere safe and lawful.

**Remember:** Protect attention before it is fully occupied.

### M07-L04 · Change the plan when risk grows

**Your goal:** Reassess a trip when weather, darkness, delays or passenger pressure accumulate.

**Learning approach.** At a meaningful change—worsening visibility, an unexpected closure, a missed rest opportunity—review the whole plan. Ask what has changed in the driver, vehicle, route and time available. A manageable single difficulty may become demanding when several occur together. This is a planning exercise, not a formula that assigns a numerical risk score.

Choose between continuing with a suitable margin, stopping safely, changing route, changing driver if lawful and suitable, or cancelling the journey. The choice depends on the actual conditions. A driver unable to maintain safe control must not continue merely because the destination is close. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§1 and 14][traffic-law]

The syllabus treats route choice, timing, passengers, fatigue and personal motives as parts of driving competence. Use the same questions after a journey: Which warning did I notice early? Which did I explain away because I wanted to finish? [Syllabus, chapters 4–5][syllabus]

**Worked decision.** A late appointment leaves a driver facing an unfamiliar route in darkness and worsening weather. Instead of protecting the original arrival time, the driver finds a safe stopping place, tells the person waiting and chooses a realistic alternative. The change happens while there is still time to make it calmly.

**Knowledge check.** Is deciding to cancel evidence that the original plan failed? **Answer:** It may show that reassessment worked. The aim is a safe decision under current conditions, not loyalty to an earlier plan.

**Remember:** Replanning is a driving skill.

### M07-R · Review and self-assessment

**Scenario:** You leave in daylight but meet heavy rain, darkness and a delay. A passenger wants to keep going without a break.

Explain changes to lighting, speed, space and the journey itself. **Review guide:** Check what you can see, what grip you can reasonably expect and whether you can concentrate. Do not compensate for delay by reducing margins.

Record the earliest sign that would make you change this trip. Name the consequence of ignoring it and one action you can prepare in advance. [Syllabus self-assessment, chapters 2–4 §3][syllabus]

## 8. Take responsibility for the vehicle and load

Permission to drive, technical capacity and actual loading are separate checks. Passing one does not mean the others are satisfied.

### M08-L01 · Understand what you may drive

**Your goal:** Use entitlement and vehicle information to assess a car or trailer combination.

**Requirements.** Ordinary category B permits a passenger car or light goods vehicle with a total weight of no more than 3,500 kg. For trailers, either the trailer's total weight is at most 750 kg, or, if above 750 kg, the car and trailer's combined total weights must not exceed 3,500 kg. The first route can therefore permit a combination up to 4,250 kg; it does not mean every 4,250 kg combination is permitted on ordinary B. [Category B][b] · [Car with trailer][trailer]

Extended B (B96) permits combined total weights up to 4,250 kg, without increasing the car's 3,500 kg limit. Under the ordinary current BE rule, the towing car is still limited to 3,500 kg total weight and the trailer or trailers may have combined total weights up to 3,500 kg. Older entitlements and conditions can differ; check the individual licence rather than applying a current-new-licence example to everyone. This book is not a full B96 or BE course. [Trailer entitlement guide][trailer-guide]

**Two tests.** Entitlement uses registered total weights. Technical towing limits also restrict what the particular car may pull in its actual loaded state. Emptying a trailer may reduce its actual weight without changing its registered total weight, so it may solve a technical-weight problem but not an entitlement problem. [Towing weights][towing]

**Vehicle comparison · Light lorry without a trailer.** A light lorry (*lätt lastbil*) has a registered total weight of at most 3,500 kg. Without a trailer, it normally follows the road's maximum speed; the general heavy-lorry cap does not apply. A heavy lorry exceeds 3,500 kg: without a trailer its vehicle maximum is 90 km/h on a motorway or expressway, and 80 km/h on other roads; with a trailer it is 80 km/h. A lower road limit always applies. For a light lorry towing, check the combination rules in M08-L02. [Transportstyrelsen: light and heavy lorries][light-lorry]

**Apply the limit.** A light lorry without a trailer on a road signed 100 km/h does not acquire an 80 km/h cap simply because it is called a lorry. Still reduce speed for visibility, grip, traffic and load; a maximum is not a target. [Safe-speed duty, chapter 3 §14][traffic-law]

**Worked decision.** A car has a total weight of 2,200 kg and a trailer 1,500 kg. Their combined total weight is 3,700 kg, and the trailer exceeds 750 kg. Ordinary B does not cover this combination. B96 may satisfy the entitlement part, but the car's towing capacity and loaded weights still need checking.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Is ordinary B automatically excluded for a 3,500 kg car and 750 kg trailer? (2) Which maximum normally applies to a light lorry without a trailer, and is it a target? **Answer:** (1) No. The at-most-750 kg trailer route can cover it; technical/actual-weight limits still apply. (2) The road maximum, with speed reduced for conditions. It is not the general heavy-lorry cap or a target; towing needs a separate combination check.

**Remember:** Check the licence first, then the vehicle and load—not one instead of the other.

### M08-L02 · Make the load part of the safety check

**Your goal:** Distinguish weight terms and assess securing, capacity and trailer suitability.

| Term | Meaning for this lesson |
|---|---|
| *Tjänstevikt* | Vehicle in its defined ready-for-use state; a car's figure includes the driver, while a trailer's does not include cargo. |
| *Totalvikt* | Registered maximum weight, built from the defined unladen/ready weight and maximum load. |
| *Bruttovikt* | Actual weight transmitted to the road at that time. |
| *Maximilast* | Total weight minus the defined ready weight: the maximum passenger/cargo load. |

Use the official definitions when reading registration data. Do not count the driver twice in an exercise that starts from a car's defined *tjänstevikt*, and do not assume a paper figure measures the load you have actually added. [Weight definitions][weights]

**Requirements.** Keep within the car's permitted trailer weight and maximum actual combined weight as well as each vehicle's limits. Registration fields O.1/O.2 identify relevant braked/unbraked trailer limits; F.3 identifies the maximum combined actual weight. Missing or unclear data needs resolution before towing. [Towing weights][towing]

Secure cargo so it cannot endanger people, fall, drag, obstruct control or obscure required lights and signals. Put heavy items low and suitably supported, and follow the equipment's rated load and securing instructions. A roof box is not permission to exceed a roof or vehicle limit. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§78–81][traffic-law] · [Safe loading][load-advice]

Projecting cargo needs separate checks. Mark cargo extending ahead or more than 1 m behind, with the specified exception for clearly visible front projection below 1 m. Darkness or poor visibility requires the prescribed lamps and reflectors, white forward and red rearward. Marking does not authorise an otherwise prohibited width, length or load. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§82–83 and chapter 4 §§15–17][traffic-law]

On roads other than private roads, the ordinary width/load rule restricts a motor vehicle or its trailer if the load projects more than 20 cm beyond either side, or the overall width including load exceeds 260 cm. The general length ceiling for a non-bus vehicle or combination is 24.0 m including load; special regimes and exemptions exist. Do not treat those general ceilings as permission to exceed a lower vehicle, route or sign limit. Special oversized transport needs its own verification. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 4 §§15–17][traffic-law]

For a conventional light braked trailer with rigid drawbar and suitable overrun brake, the maximum is normally 80 km/h. For an unbraked trailer up to 750 kg, 80 km/h requires its total weight to be no more than half the car’s defined ready weight; when the trailer is empty, its ready weight is used for this comparison instead. The ordinary unbraked combination outside those conditions is limited to 40 km/h, subject to the other combination requirements; other configurations can be limited to 30. Use Transportstyrelsen's calculator and guidance for the specific combination, and obey any lower road limit. [Trailer guide: speed conditions][trailer-guide] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 4 §20, version applicable on 1 October 2026][traffic-law]

**Towball load · A separate force.** Towball load (*kultryck*) is the trailer's downward load at the coupling, not the trailer's whole loaded weight. With a conventional towball behind the car's rear axle, excessive downward force acts through a lever arm: it increases rear loading while reducing the load carried by the front axle. Front unloading does not require the wheels to leave the road. With less front-tyre loading, available steering grip and response can be impaired. [Official towing guide: front-wheel unloading, page 6][towball-physics]

Trafikverket warns that incorrect coupling pressure can lift the car or trailer. Follow the specified towball, coupling, axle and vehicle limits for the actual combination; there is no universal number in this lesson. Measure and redistribute the load as instructed before travelling. Total trailer weight, coupling load and cargo securing are separate checks. Do not cure excessive downward load by guessing or creating an equally unsuitable load distribution. [Trafikverket: coupling-load caution][load-advice] · [Official towing guide: ratings and load distribution][towball-physics]

**Worked loading decision.** Your total trailer weight is within the car's towing limit, but a heavy object far forward makes the coupling load excessive. The total-weight check has not proved suitable axle loading. Stop preparation, establish the specified limits and adjust and secure the load before driving.

**Worked decision.** A trailer's ready weight is 400 kg and cargo adds 500 kg, giving an illustrative actual weight of 900 kg. A car rated to tow only 800 kg cannot pull that load merely because the driver's entitlement covers the registered total weights.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Does a strap prove the vehicle is within its weight limit? (2) Can an acceptable total trailer weight still conceal excessive towball load, and what can that do? **Answer:** (1) No. Weight and securing are separate checks; inspect coupling, lights, tyres and equipment too. (2) Yes. Excess downward load behind the rear axle can unload the front axle and impair steering grip. Check the actual coupling/axle limits and load distribution before driving.

**Remember:** Establish what the load weighs, where it is, how it is restrained and what limits apply.

### M08-L03 · Keep the car legally ready

**Your goal:** Build an ownership checklist for registration, insurance, inspection and vehicle status.

**Requirements.** The registered owner of an on-road car must have compulsory motor insurance. Not using the car, or having a defect or driving prohibition, does not by itself end that obligation; formal off-road status matters. Compulsory insurance covers specified traffic injuries and damage to others, not every loss to your own car. Optional cover varies by policy. [Compulsory motor insurance][insurance]

For an ordinary car or light goods vehicle up to 3,500 kg, the normal inspection sequence is: first by 36 months after the month of first use, second by 24 months after the first inspection month, then by 14 months after the preceding inspection month. Check the recorded date and any special category or exemption for your vehicle. An earlier inspection changes the next calculation; an old reminder is not proof of the current deadline. [Inspection intervals][inspection]

An on-road registration status, valid insurance and completed inspection answer different questions. Check the vehicle's status and any prohibition before use, and deal with tax obligations through the current official service. A car being physically driveable does not establish legal readiness. [On-road and off-road status][status]

**Ownership process.** Transportstyrelsen currently provides eligible private-person transfers through Mina sidor; other cases may require the latest registration certificate part 2. The service has eligibility conditions. Consult the current instructions instead of relying on an old app or a remembered screen sequence. Part 1 and the vehicle enquiry service provide technical information; protect ownership documents appropriately. [Ownership transfer][owner] · [Registration certificate][registration]

**Inspection deadline · An automatic ban.** If the required periodic inspection deadline is missed, an automatic driving ban (*inträtt körförbud*) applies. You remain responsible even without a reminder. Booking a later appointment does not end the ban. The deadline ban lasts until the inspection has been carried out; follow its result, since defects can bring a new prohibition or a repair/reinspection requirement. Check the vehicle record before ordinary use. [Transportstyrelsen: missed inspection][inspection-ban] · [Vehicle Regulation, chapter 6 §§15–21][vehicle-inspection-law]

**Restricted trips.** With an overdue-inspection ban and on-road status (*påställd*), only the shortest suitable route to a repair workshop for repair or to an inspection business for inspection is permitted. If the vehicle is off-road (*avställd*) and the ban still applies, the exception is the shortest suitable route to the nearest inspection business. Traffic insurance and other applicable requirements still matter; the off-road exception requires no unpaid vehicle taxes. It does not permit errands or commuting. A prohibition issued because the vehicle is dangerously defective has different conditions: it must be recovered from the place where that prohibition is issued. [Transportstyrelsen: inspection prohibitions][inspection-ban] · [Transportstyrelsen: off-road inspection conditions][off-road-inspection]

**Worked deadline.** An ordinary light car's recorded inspection deadline is 31 October 2026. Without the required inspection, the deadline ban starts on 1 November. An appointment on 5 November does not allow normal driving on 2 November. Arrange a permitted inspection trip, or transport if it cannot be driven safely and lawfully, then follow the inspection result. These dates illustrate the rule; use the actual vehicle's record. [Vehicle Regulation, chapter 6 §§15 and 21][vehicle-inspection-law]

**Worked decision.** A buyer collects an on-road car but assumes the seller's insurance is enough for the trip home. The buyer checks the transfer and arranges the required cover from the applicable ownership date before driving. The sale does not transfer the seller's insurance as personal cover for the buyer.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Does garaging an on-road car automatically end compulsory insurance? (2) The inspection deadline passed, but you booked an appointment. May you commute meanwhile? **Answer:** (1) No. Formal status and insurance rules apply. (2) No. The automatic deadline ban continues until inspection is carried out. Only the applicable restricted inspection/repair trip is permitted; vehicle status, insurance, tax and safety conditions must be satisfied. Follow the inspection result and check for any further prohibition before ordinary use.

**Remember:** Keep a record of dates and obligations, then verify them against the current vehicle record.

### M08-L04 · Know what assistance cannot do

**Your goal:** Explain why safety systems and powertrain differences do not remove your duties to observe and control.

Trafikverket describes modern protection as including crashworthiness and systems such as lane assistance and automatic emergency braking. These functions can help, but a good safety rating is not a promise that any collision can be avoided. Recent official crash testing also shows the limits of automatic braking in severe truck-trailer collision scenarios. [Crash safety][crash] · [Trafikverket: braking and truck-trailer tests][aeb]

ABS is anti-lock braking. It helps retain the ability to develop steering and braking forces together, within available friction. Stability control reduces the risk of skid crashes; it does not create extra road grip or excuse excessive speed. The driver still has to maintain control and an appropriate speed. [VTI friction report, section 4.3][friction] · [Trafikverket: tyre and stability advice][tyre-advice] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §14][traffic-law]

**Powertrain awareness.** A plug-in hybrid can operate on electricity and with a combustion engine. That difference matters when interpreting fuel use and emissions: do not assume that every journey is electrically powered. [Transportstyrelsen: plug-in hybrids][hybrid]

For the actual vehicle, establish how it becomes ready to move, how drive and reverse are selected, how it is secured, and what warnings or assistance limitations need attention. Do not transfer a universal gear-change, engine-speed, coasting or brake-check recipe between manual, automatic, hybrid and electric vehicles. Vehicle-specific procedures need the vehicle's own instructions and practical teaching. The curriculum's role is to help you identify those questions, not guess their answers.

**Worked decision.** A driver assumes lane assistance will handle a temporary roadworks layout. Instead, they must read the actual signs and lane path, stay in control and understand the system's limitations. An assistance symbol does not transfer responsibility for the route.

**Knowledge check.** Does an anti-lock system guarantee a short stop on every surface? **Answer:** No. It operates within the friction available, and stopping still depends on speed, surface, tyres and conditions.

**Remember:** Know what the system assists, what it cannot guarantee and what remains your task.

### M08-R · Review and self-assessment

**Scenario:** You borrow a car and trailer for a move. You know the registration numbers but not the actual load weight or towing limit.

Explain the checks required before agreeing to drive. **Review guide:** Establish entitlement, registered and actual weights, technical limits, vehicle status, insurance, inspection, coupling, tyres, lights and securing. Do not let one successful calculator result stand in for every physical check.

## 9. Reduce the impact of each journey

Environmental responsibility starts before choosing a fuel or a driving technique. Consider whether the journey is needed, which travel mode suits it and how to make a necessary drive safely and efficiently.

### M09-L01 · Look beyond the exhaust

**Your goal:** Compare climate, air-quality and noise effects without assuming that a vehicle has no environmental impact.

Transportstyrelsen distinguishes several effects. Greenhouse-gas emissions relate to the amount of travel, fuels, energy efficiency and electrification. Air pollution includes exhaust emissions and wear particles from brakes, tyres and the road. Noise is another effect, with tyre-road noise becoming important as speed rises. These are different measures: improving one does not prove that every environmental effect has disappeared. [Climate and energy][climate] · [Air quality][air] · [Noise][noise]

**Learning approach.** Ask what a comparison includes. Does an emissions figure describe a test result, the actual journey or a broader lifecycle? Are you comparing equal distances and occupancy? If the basis is missing, avoid a confident numerical conclusion. This lesson does not assign a universal emissions saving to a powertrain or declare a whole journey “zero impact.”

A plug-in hybrid combines electrical and combustion-engine operation. Transportstyrelsen changed the weighting used for new registrations in 2026 to better reflect the mix of those modes. The general lesson is to understand what a reported figure represents; it is not a promise about your next journey's consumption. [Transportstyrelsen: plug-in-hybrid weighting][hybrid]

**Emissions explained · Fossil carbon and a cold catalyst.** Fossil petrol contains carbon stored for millions of years. Burning it combines that carbon with oxygen and releases CO₂ into the atmosphere. The added CO₂ strengthens the natural greenhouse effect and contributes to warming. [Long-stored carbon: Naturvårdsverket][fossil-carbon] · [Climate effect: Trafikverket][climate-effect]

A conventional petrol car's three-way catalyst reduces carbon monoxide, hydrocarbons and nitrogen oxides by chemical conversion. It needs sufficient heat to become effective, so it is least effective just after a cold start. Conversion products include water and CO₂: cleaning up these pollutants does not capture the fossil carbon or cancel its climate impact. No single warm-up time applies to every car. [Transportstyrelsen: catalyst operation, page 24][catalyst-warmup] · [Trafikverket: cold emission controls][road-air]

**Pollutant comparison.** Keep CO distinct from CO₂. The table summarises different mechanisms; one cleaner exhaust measure cannot stand for all of them.

| Substance | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| CO₂ · carbon dioxide | Added fossil CO₂ strengthens the greenhouse effect. |
| CO · carbon monoxide | Binds strongly to haemoglobin and interferes with the blood's oxygen transport. |
| NOx · nitrogen oxides | Can irritate airways, contribute to acidification and, with hydrocarbons and sunlight, form ground-level ozone. |
| Traffic particles | Exhaust and wear particles can enter and harm the respiratory system. |

[Trafikverket: CO, NOx and particle mechanisms][road-air] · [Trafikverket: CO₂ and warming][climate-effect]

**Powertrain and washing · Check the boundary.** A battery-electric car uses only electricity for propulsion and has no combustion tailpipe exhaust while driving. That does not remove impacts from vehicle/battery production, raw-material extraction or electricity supply. Tyre and road wear still matter. A plug-in hybrid combines electric and combustion operation and its traction battery can be charged externally. A conventional non-plug-in hybrid cannot be charged from an external electricity supply. [Trafikverket: electric cars and hybrid types][electric-types]

Choose a purpose-built automatic or self-service wash that collects and treats its wastewater. Washing on an ordinary street or driveway can carry oil, metals and cleaning chemicals into ground or surface water. Treatment reduces the problem but does not remove every pollutant. Follow the facility's product instructions and local water-protection rules. [Göteborg environmental authority: washing and treatment][wash-water]

**Apply the comparison.** A catalyst-equipped petrol car still releases fossil CO₂. An electric car avoids combustion exhaust on that drive, but needs a broader production-and-energy comparison. For either car, choose washing by how the dirty water is handled, rather than assuming a mild detergent makes street runoff harmless.

**Worked decision.** Two vehicles are advertised using different environmental measures. One figure describes exhaust emissions under a test procedure; another describes noise. The learner does not rank them as though both figures measure the same thing. They first identify the question being answered and the information still missing.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Why does fossil petrol warm the climate, even with a catalyst, and when is that catalyst least effective? (2) Match CO, NOx and particles to their effects. (3) Does an electric car have no environmental impact; which hybrid can charge externally? (4) Where should washing water go? **Answer:** (1) Long-stored carbon becomes CO₂, strengthening the greenhouse effect. A catalyst converts other pollutants and works poorly just after a cold start. (2) CO impairs blood oxygen transport; NOx irritates airways and contributes to acidification and ground-level ozone; particles harm airways. (3) No: production and electricity supply still matter. A plug-in hybrid can charge externally; a non-plug-in hybrid cannot. (4) Choose a purpose-built wash collecting and treating wastewater.

**Remember:** Name the effect and the basis of comparison before making the claim.

### M09-L02 · Use energy with foresight

**Your goal:** Plan anticipatory driving that reduces unnecessary energy use while preserving safety.

Transportstyrelsen recommends correct tyre pressure, observing speed limits and removing unused roof racks or roof boxes. Driving technique and environmental awareness are part of driver education and assessment. [Transportstyrelsen: economical driving][eco]

**Learning approach.** Look ahead and respond early to a developing queue rather than accelerating towards it and then braking sharply. Choose a suitable speed and preserve a gap that lets you adapt smoothly. This is not an instruction to delay necessary braking: safe control and traffic duties come first. If you need to stop for a person, do so regardless of what an efficiency display suggests. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§2 and 14][traffic-law]

Prepare the vehicle for the trip. Check the specified tyre pressure and remove unnecessary external carrying equipment when it is no longer needed. Combine these habits with realistic route and timing decisions. Avoid claiming a fixed percentage saving: load, road, weather, vehicle and technique all affect the outcome.

**Vehicle-specific boundary.** Gear selection and deceleration strategies vary. Do not use a fixed engine-speed rule for every car or assume every powertrain cuts fuel or recovers energy in the same way. Learn the actual vehicle's approved operation with your instructor. This book does not recommend coasting in neutral or switching the vehicle off while moving.

**Practical energy choices · Prepare and control the car.** Use a timer with an installed electric engine heater so it warms the engine when needed, without running unnecessarily long. Set it for the intended departure using the equipment instructions and conditions; there is no universal heating duration. [Gagnef municipal energy advice: heater timer][heater-timer]

Air conditioning consumes energy, but it can support comfort, alertness and clear windows. Avoid unnecessary cooling while keeping those safety benefits. Use the car's appropriate demisting controls; visibility takes priority over a saving. Cooling is not a remedy for sleep deprivation: stop and rest if drowsy. Revisit M07-L01 for seeing clearly and M07-L03 for attention. [NZ Transport Agency: cooling, alertness and demisting][cooling-safety]

In a conventional manual car, choose a suitably high gear in which the engine runs smoothly without labouring. Change down if the load or gradient makes that gear unsuitable. Engine braking means slowing with the accelerator released while a suitable gear remains engaged; do not disengage the drive by pressing the clutch or selecting neutral for this technique. Use the brakes whenever safety requires. Actual engine control varies, so this definition does not promise zero fuel use or a universal RPM. [Official vehicle agency: comfortable gear choice and in-gear deceleration][manual-eco]

Unnecessary carried mass increases the demands on the vehicle. Unload heavy equipment you no longer need before future journeys, while retaining necessary safety and access equipment. Removing cargo is a different check from removing a roof box's air resistance. [Swedish public energy advice: minimise weight][carried-mass]

**Apply before departure.** Remove unused heavy equipment, schedule the heater for the trip, and keep effective demisting. On an uphill section, use the gear the car can comfortably handle; a high gear that strains the engine is not the intended economical technique.

**Fuel arithmetic · Understand the unit.** A consumption rate of 6.5 litres per 100 km means 6.5 litres for each 100 km under the supplied or measured conditions. A 200 km journey at the same assumed rate uses twice as much, while 50 km uses half as much. Actual consumption varies with vehicle, load, road, weather and driving. [Transportstyrelsen: economical driving][eco] supplies the driving context; the equations below are editorial arithmetic derived from the unit, not agency-prescribed formulas.

**Journey consumption:** journey litres = distance in km × consumption in litres/100 km ÷ 100. Divide distance by 100 to find the number of 100 km portions, then multiply by the supplied rate. The kilometres cancel, leaving litres. Retain decimal working and round only the final result at the precision requested.

**Consumption example · 120 km at 6.5 litres/100 km.** The journey contains 120 ÷ 100 = 1.2 portions of 100 km. Consumption = 1.2 × 6.5 = **7.8 litres**. Do not multiply distance by the rate without dividing by 100; that would misread the unit.

**Savings from a supplied reduction:** litres saved = distance in km × reduction in litres/100 km ÷ 100. If two consumption rates are supplied, subtract the lower rate from the higher first. Multiply that difference by the number of 100 km portions. A supplied saving is an exercise assumption, not a guaranteed benefit of a particular driving technique.

**Savings example · A reduction of 1.5 litres/100 km over 300 km.** The journey has 300 ÷ 100 = 3 portions. Savings = 3 × 1.5 = **4.5 litres**. This is the difference in litres used, not the new total consumption. You need the original or new consumption rate to calculate either total.

**Two-rate example · 7.0 and 6.5 litres/100 km over 240 km.** First find the rate difference: 7.0 − 6.5 = 0.5 litres/100 km. Savings = 240 × 0.5 ÷ 100 = **1.2 litres**. The original journey uses 16.8 litres and the lower-rate journey 15.6 litres; their difference is also 1.2 litres. No percentage-saving promise is implied.

**Precision and midpoint rounding · Keep the exact result visible.** Some supplied savings exercises display one-decimal answers without specifying precision or a rounding convention in their prompts. Four such examples below use **ties-to-even** for the displayed keyed value. This rounds to the nearest tenth; at an exact halfway value, choose the tenth whose retained digit is even. It differs from rounding a halfway value upwards. Do not call a rounded value the exact amount, or silently apply one convention where another is intended.

**Four midpoint examples.** A reduction of 0.5 litres/100 km over 150 km gives exactly 0.75 litres, displayed as 0.8 to one decimal using ties-to-even. A reduction of 1.5 over 150 km gives exactly 2.25 litres, displayed as 2.2. A reduction of 0.5 over 250 km gives exactly 1.25 litres, displayed as 1.2. A reduction of 1.5 over 250 km gives exactly 3.75 litres, displayed as 3.8. The units for every reduction are litres/100 km.

**Compare conventions.** With ordinary half-up rounding to one decimal, 2.25 becomes 2.3 and 1.25 becomes 1.3; ties-to-even gives 2.2 and 1.2. The exact products remain 2.25 and 1.25 litres. For these supplied examples, keep the exact arithmetic and the disclosed displayed-answer convention separate. For a new problem, follow its stated precision/convention; if they are absent, report the exact result and recognise that a rounded answer needs an assumption. Round once at the end, not the rate or intermediate product.

**Try the fuel arithmetic.** (A) Find journey consumption for 80 km at 6.5 litres/100 km. (B) Find the saving from a supplied reduction of 0.5 litres/100 km over 250 km, first exactly and then to one decimal using ties-to-even. (C) Find the saving over 200 km when supplied consumption falls from 7.5 to 6.2 litres/100 km. Give your units and show the rate difference where needed. Answers are in the separate lesson answer section.

**Worked decision.** You can see that a traffic queue is stationary ahead. You check behind and begin a controlled reduction in speed early, retaining enough room to stop. You do not accelerate simply to close the gap, and you do not roll into a crossing to preserve momentum.

**Knowledge check.** (1) May economy override a necessary stop? (2) How should you use an engine-heater timer and cooling? (3) What is a suitable high gear and engine braking in a manual car? (4) What should happen to unneeded heavy cargo? (5) Complete the three fuel-arithmetic exercises above, including exact and rounded values for B. **Answer:** (1) No. (2) Heat when needed, avoiding unnecessary runtime; retain comfort, alertness and clear windows without needless cooling. (3) Use a gear allowing smooth running without strain. Engine braking releases the accelerator with a suitable gear engaged; brake as needed. No universal RPM or zero-fuel promise. (4) Unload it before future trips; retain necessary equipment. (5) A: 80 × 6.5 ÷ 100 = 5.2 litres. B: 250 × 0.5 ÷ 100 = exactly 1.25 litres; ties-to-even to one decimal gives 1.2 litres. C: the rate difference is 7.5 − 6.2 = 1.3 litres/100 km; 200 × 1.3 ÷ 100 = 2.6 litres saved. These are supplied exercise rates, not guaranteed technique savings.

**Remember:** Reduce avoidable demands on the car while keeping full control of the journey.

### M09-L03 · Choose the journey as well as the car

**Your goal:** Compare combining trips, walking, cycling, public transport and driving for a realistic personal journey.

The syllabus includes transport choices and the way personal circumstances influence them. A useful comparison recognises that people's access needs, schedules and available services differ. [Category B syllabus, chapters 4–5][syllabus]

**Learning approach.** Start with the purpose: reaching work, collecting goods, accompanying a person or making a visit. Then list workable options. Could two errands be combined? Could a nearby trip be walked or cycled safely? Is public transport available for both directions? If you share a car journey, can everyone use the required protection and travel without creating a rushed schedule?

Do not replace one unrealistic assumption with another. A route may be short but inaccessible to a particular person. A service may run outward but not at the return time. A car may solve those needs, while a different choice suits the following day's journey. The exercise is to make the choice deliberately.

**Worked decision.** A learner normally makes two separate drives to the same district. On a day when both appointments can be combined without leaving anyone waiting unsafely, the learner plans one return trip. No exact fuel saving is claimed; the decision avoids a duplicated journey.

**Knowledge check.** Is there one transport mode that is automatically best for every journey and every person? **Answer:** No. Compare the actual needs, conditions and available options, then avoid unnecessary travel where practical.

**Remember:** Good planning includes asking whether to drive, not only how to drive.

### M09-L04 · Check where the vehicle can go

**Your goal:** Find official environmental-zone information and determine what to verify before entering.

**Requirements.** Municipalities can introduce environmental zones under the national framework. Class 1 concerns heavy buses and heavy goods vehicles; class 2 concerns passenger cars and light buses/goods vehicles. For class 2, spark-ignition vehicles need Euro 5 or Euro 6 and diesel vehicles need Euro 6. For light vehicles, class 3 ordinarily permits only electric, fuel-cell or qualifying gas vehicles; gas vehicles must meet the specified Euro 6 emissions criteria. A light plug-in hybrid is not admitted merely because it can sometimes run electrically. Exemptions exist for specified vehicles and uses. [Transportstyrelsen: environmental zones][zones] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 4 §§22–24a][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Check three layers: the national vehicle criteria, the current local restriction and your actual vehicle record. A national possibility to introduce a zone does not mean every city has introduced it. A manufacturer's marketing name does not prove the vehicle's recorded emissions class. Locate the applicable decision and signs for the actual route.

Transportstyrelsen's official traffic-regulation collection, STFS, provides local regulations. Check whether a decision is in force on the date of travel, and whether another decision has changed it. Do not rely on a remembered map or an announcement of a proposed zone. [Official local traffic regulations][stfs]

**Studded tyres · A separate local restriction.** Studs wear the road surface and create particles that can become airborne and enter the airways. Municipalities can restrict studded vehicles on particular roads or areas to address this problem. Check the current local decision in STFS, its dates, extent, exceptions and signs for your route. National permission to use studs in a season does not override a local prohibition. C44 denotes this restriction for motor vehicles other than moped class II; a supplementary panel can include that class too. Revisit the official C44 example in M07-L02. [Trafikverket: stud-related particles][road-air] · [Transportstyrelsen: local restrictions, page 13][studs-local] · [Transportstyrelsen: C44][studs-sign]

**Worked decision.** A driver has heard that a city plans a new zone. Before travelling, they verify the operative restriction and compare it with the car's recorded category. The route is chosen from current information, not from a headline remembered months earlier.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Does “hybrid” guarantee environmental-zone access? (2) Why can a municipality restrict studded tyres, and what must you check? **Answer:** (1) No. Check zone class, vehicle criteria, exemptions and current decision. (2) Studs wear roads and create airborne particles. Check the local decision, route, dates, exceptions and signs; national permitted season does not override a local ban.

**Remember:** A rule's location and effective date are part of the rule.

### M09-R · Review and self-assessment

**Scenario:** Plan a regular journey with an optional errand, two possible modes and a destination near a restricted area.

Explain your choice without inventing savings figures. **Review guide:** Compare realistic alternatives, avoid unnecessary distance, identify restrictions and keep safety ahead of efficiency during any drive.

Record one assumption you made about environmental impact or access. Identify what evidence would change your decision and where you will verify it. [Syllabus self-assessment, chapters 2, 4 and 5 §3][syllabus]

## 10. Respond, reflect and keep learning

When a journey is interrupted, the setting determines the safest response. Afterwards, use what happened to identify a specific learning need rather than simply deciding that you were lucky or unlucky.

### M10-L01 · When the car cannot continue

**Your goal:** Compare a breakdown on an ordinary road, a motorway and in a tunnel.

**Requirement.** If a breakdown leaves a vehicle where stopping is prohibited, it must be moved to a suitable place as soon as possible, subject to the special rules for an accident scene. Warning obligations depend on the situation. A warning triangle must give approaching drivers enough warning; the law does not prescribe one universal distance for every road. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§51 and 57][traffic-law]

**Official motorway/2+1-road advice.** If possible, reach the safest available stopping place. Switch on hazard lights, warn other traffic and leave the vehicle and carriageway promptly. Move away from traffic, outside a barrier where one exists. Trafikverket recommends placing the triangle at least 100 m away, preferably farther, and taking care while doing so, walking at the side and outside the barrier where possible. Do not expose yourself in the traffic lane to carry out a roadside repair. Call professional recovery; call 112 when life is in danger. [Trafikverket: breakdown advice][breakdown]

**Tunnel advice is different.** Use hazard lights, move towards the edge or an emergency bay if possible and switch off the engine. For a non-urgent incident, Trafikverket advises staying in the car or protected from traffic close to it while calling for assistance. In an urgent situation, leave for the nearest emergency exit and use the assistance point, emergency phone or 112. In a fire, leave and reach an emergency exit; follow signs, announcements and emergency personnel. Do not turn a non-urgent-tunnel recommendation into advice to stay beside a fire. [Trafikverket: tunnels][tunnel]

**Learning approach.** On an ordinary road, assess traffic, visibility and the location before choosing a protected waiting place and arranging help. Do not assume that a procedure for a sheltered bay, an exposed motorway shoulder and a smoke-filled tunnel can be identical.

**Overheating · Leave the hot cap closed.** In a conventional pressurised cooling system, hot coolant and steam can remain under pressure even after the engine is switched off. Opening the radiator cap, or the cap of a pressurised coolant reservoir, can release that pressure and expel scalding liquid or steam, causing severe burns. Never loosen or remove the cap while the engine or cooling system is hot. Switching off is not proof that pressure and heat have disappeared. [Manufacturer handbook: hot coolant and pressure warning][hot-coolant]

Treat a temperature warning as a reason to respond according to the actual vehicle handbook, not as an invitation to open a cap. Revisit M02-L01 for warning indicators and fault recognition. Protect people from traffic first and arrange professional assistance where needed. Cooling-system layouts and stopping instructions vary; this lesson gives no fixed waiting time, cap-opening sequence or roadside refill procedure. [Manufacturer cooling-system precautions][hot-coolant] · [Trafikverket: protect people and call recovery][breakdown]

**Apply the warning.** The temperature warning appears on a motorway journey. After reaching the safest available stopping place as conditions allow, follow the vehicle's stopping instructions and this lesson's motorway scene-safety advice. A passenger suggests opening the coolant cap immediately because the engine is now off. Reject that action: residual heat and pressure can still cause a burn. Call recovery from a protected position rather than attempting an exposed repair. If the event is in a tunnel, use the tunnel response above; smoke, fire or danger to life requires the urgent response, not waiting beside the vehicle to diagnose it. [Trafikverket: motorway breakdown][breakdown] · [Trafikverket: tunnel urgency][tunnel]

**Worked decision.** A driver with a puncture on a motorway wants to change the wheel immediately. They first protect people from traffic and arrange recovery rather than working in an exposed position. A warning triangle does not create a protected workshop.

**Knowledge check.** (1) Is one identical waiting rule suitable for every breakdown? (2) Why must you leave a hot coolant cap closed even after switching off the engine? **Answer:** (1) No. Apply the guidance for the location and urgency, with people’s safety first. (2) Heat and pressure can remain; opening the cap can release scalding coolant or steam and cause severe burns. Do not open it while hot. Follow the actual handbook and arrange assistance; no fixed waiting time proves it safe.

**Remember:** Establish where the danger is before deciding where to wait.

### M10-L02 · When someone may be hurt

**Your goal:** Identify scene-safety, emergency-contact and first-aid learning needs following a collision.

**Requirements.** If involved in a traffic accident, remain at the scene, help injured people to the extent of your ability and assist with necessary measures. Provide your name, address and information when requested by another involved person or someone whose property was damaged. Notify the owner or police promptly if damaged property has nobody present to receive the information. Notify police promptly for a personal injury that is not insignificant. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 2 §8][traffic-law]

A vehicle creating danger or obstruction after a collision should be moved to a suitable place. However, where someone has died or been seriously injured, move it only if it endangers other traffic, and preserve evidence as required. Staying at the scene does not mean standing in an active traffic lane. Protect yourself and others while arranging help. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 2 §8][traffic-law]

**Emergency response.** At danger to life, call 112. Give the location, direction of travel, what happened and what dangers you can see; follow the operator's instructions. Make observations from a safe position rather than walking into moving traffic to gather details. [Trafikverket: emergency contact][breakdown]

**First-aid reference.** Use [1177's first-aid guidance][first-aid] to learn more about helping someone in an acute situation, including when a person is unconscious or has breathing difficulties. Practical first-aid training can help you prepare. This lesson is deliberately brief and does not reproduce clinical procedures or a pulse-check sequence. In an emergency, call 112 rather than spending time searching a website.

**Wildlife collisions.** Trafikverket advises calling 112 and marking the collision location safely so responders can find it, even if the animal has left. Avoid approaching an injured animal. Its guidance gives a separate precaution for wild boar, bear, lynx and wolf: continue 200 m before stopping and marking, then call police immediately. Only move the vehicle when safe and possible; explain the actual collision location and the animal's direction clearly. This is agency safety guidance, not a claim that every animal species has identical statutory reporting rules. [Trafikverket: wildlife collisions][wildlife]

**Worked decision.** After a collision, a person is badly injured and vehicles are still passing. You call 112 and describe the immediate traffic danger from a protected position, following instructions and helping within your ability. Photographing damage is not the first priority.

**Knowledge check.** Should you leave a collision you were involved in simply because you believe you were not at fault? **Answer:** No. The duty to remain and assist applies regardless of fault.

**Remember:** Protect people, summon help and cooperate. Learn detailed first aid from the dedicated healthcare guidance and training.

### M10-L03 · Explain a whole journey

**Your goal:** Review a mixed journey, justify decisions, identify missed hazards and propose safer alternatives.

This exercise brings the earlier lessons together. It is an original learning scenario, not an official test question or a court case.

**Scenario.** You plan to take a passenger to a rural appointment using an unfamiliar car. The outward journey includes a residential area, a roundabout and a faster road. Rain is expected later. At the destination, the nearest empty space is before a pedestrian crossing. On the return journey you become delayed; the passenger asks you to pass a slower vehicle near a bend.

**Work through the decisions.**

1. Before leaving, establish fitness, entitlement, vehicle readiness, passenger protection and the controls you may need. Check the route and alternatives.
2. In the residential area, identify crossings, people hidden by parked vehicles and the applicable speed. At the roundabout, separate entry, lane and exit decisions.
3. On the faster section, preserve a following gap and a view ahead. If the weather worsens, reassess both lighting and speed.
4. At the destination, apply the stopping restrictions before considering convenience. Choose a lawful place with a safe manoeuvre and passenger exit.
5. On the return trip, reject an overtaking attempt without a complete clear path. Change the arrival expectation instead of the safety margin.

The rules behind these decisions are explained in M02–M07. The duties to adapt speed, yield where required and avoid dangerous manoeuvres remain applicable throughout. [Traffic Regulation, chapters 2–3][traffic-law]

**Worked reflection.** “I noticed the bend but focused on the passenger's request. Next time I will state that I am waiting before I start looking for a gap.” This identifies a trigger and an action. “I must be more careful” does not explain what to practise.

**Knowledge check.** If no collision occurred, does that prove every decision was sound? **Answer:** No. Review whether the decision depended on luck, another person avoiding you, or a margin you could not justify.

**Remember:** Assess the decision process as well as the outcome.

### M10-L04 · Make a realistic readiness plan

**Your goal:** Compare knowledge, practical performance and confidence, then plan further learning and accessible examination preparation.

The official theory test covers vehicle knowledge/manoeuvring, environment, road safety, traffic rules and personal factors. It checks understanding across those areas; memorising the wording of practice questions is not equivalent preparation. The practical test requires you to apply knowledge through vehicle checks, manoeuvres and driving in traffic. [Trafikverket: category B tests][test] · [Practical test][practical]

**Current standard theory-test format, checked 1 October 2026:** 70 questions, of which 65 count towards the score; at least 52 points are needed to pass; standard time is 50 minutes. These administrative figures can change, so verify them before booking. They are not a recommended pass mark for this book's short learning checks. [Trafikverket: theory-test format][test]

Trafikverket provides language options, including English, and application routes for support such as extended time, oral support or an interpreter where appropriate. Support generally needs approval before the supported test can be booked. Check the current requirements and apply early enough for a decision; do not assume that support can be added at the door. [Languages][languages] · [Test support][support]

**Learning approach.** Review three kinds of evidence: Can you explain the rule and its exceptions? Can you recognise the situation without a prompt? Can you act safely and consistently during supervised practice? A high confidence rating with little evidence is a learning signal, not a readiness certificate.

Write your next practice plan in concrete terms: “Recognise cycle passages after a turn and explain the duty before reaching them,” or “Demonstrate the safety check and explain each defect response.” Revisit errors after a delay and in a changed scenario so that you are practising understanding rather than recognising an answer.

**Worked decision.** A learner answers most checks correctly but still needs reminders to scan at junctions. They continue targeted supervised practice instead of treating their theory score as proof of practical readiness.

**Knowledge check.** Can this manuscript or its checks certify that you will pass or are ready to drive alone? **Answer:** No. Use it to guide learning alongside official requirements, qualified instruction and demonstrated practical competence.

**Remember:** Readiness is supported by evidence across knowledge, judgement and practical performance.

### M10-R · Review and self-assessment

**Scenario:** Review an entire journey that included an unexpected stop and a decision you would now change.

Separate what you knew, what you noticed and what you did. **Review guide:** Identify the relevant duty, the information available at the time, a safer alternative and one practical learning action. Refer back to the appropriate module and official source.

Write a dated plan for further study or supervised practice, including how you will know that the skill has improved. Reassess again after practising; completing the book is the beginning of a continuing habit. [Syllabus self-assessment, chapters 2–5 §3][syllabus]

## Appendix A · Put the learning into practice

These are original study checklists. Use the cited lessons for the detailed rules and limitations; a tick does not certify a vehicle or driver as safe.

### Before a journey

- Establish that the driver is legally permitted and actually fit to drive. Resolve uncertainty about fatigue, medication or conditions before leaving. See M01-L01/L02.
- Check the route, weather, traffic, realistic timing, stopping opportunities and a fallback option. Include the return journey. See M01-L04 and M07-L04.
- Check the vehicle, usable visibility, required lighting and controls. Confirm protection for each passenger. See M02.
- Establish load and trailer limits, coupling and securing if relevant. Verify the vehicle's recorded status and obligations. See M08.
- Prepare navigation and passenger help before moving. Keep attention available for traffic. See M07-L03.

### While approaching a new situation

1. **Observe:** What can I see, what is hidden and who might enter?
2. **Identify the duty:** Which rule, sign, marking, signal or authorised instruction applies?
3. **Preserve space:** Can I stop or alter the plan without forcing another person to avoid me?
4. **Communicate:** Will others understand my intended movement?
5. **Recheck:** Is the information still true as I begin and complete the action?

This is an original learning routine based on the observation, risk and cooperation aims of the [category B syllabus][syllabus]. It is not an official test sequence.

### Before leaving a parked vehicle

Check that stopping and parking are permitted at that location, time and for that vehicle. Meet fee, duration and permit conditions. Secure against rolling and unauthorised use. Check for people before opening doors and provide a safe place for passengers to leave. See M05-L03/L04 and the [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§47–55][traffic-law].

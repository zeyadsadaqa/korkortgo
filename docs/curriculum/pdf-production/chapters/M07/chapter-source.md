## 7. Adapt when conditions change

A journey can become more demanding without any single dramatic event. Darkness, reduced grip, distraction and tiredness can combine. Reassess while you still have comfortable choices.

### M07-L01 · Drive within what you can see

**Your goal:** Choose a visibility and lighting strategy for darkness, fog and glare.

**Requirements.** Your speed must allow a stop within the visible road and before foreseeable hazards. Darkness does not create a separate allowance to travel beyond the distance you can assess. Keep the required lamps on; daytime running lights alone may be insufficient when conditions deteriorate. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§14–15 and 68–71][traffic-law]

Use main beam when needed for a sufficient field of view, but not where the road is adequately lit, where it can dazzle an approaching driver or an adjacent rail/ship operator, or at close distance behind another vehicle. Dipped beam is the usual driving light. In fog or heavy precipitation, front fog lights may replace dipped beam; the prohibited combinations must not be used together. A rear fog light is restricted to fog, heavy precipitation or comparable reduced visibility and must not dazzle a following driver. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§69–72][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Make lighting and speed one decision. If you dip the lights and can see less far, reassess speed immediately. Check the edges of the visible road for people and animals rather than assuming a dark area is empty. Keep windows and mirrors clear enough for a useful view and learn demisting controls before a difficult journey.

Automatic lighting is an aid, not confirmation that every required lamp is on. In a car whose daytime-light mode leaves the rear lights off, check the actual setting when darkness or bad weather arrives. [Transportstyrelsen: vehicle lighting][lights]

**Worked decision.** Rain becomes heavy during a daytime trip. The dashboard remains illuminated, but the driver checks and selects the appropriate exterior lighting rather than inferring that the rear lights must be on. The driver also reduces speed for the shorter usable view.

**Knowledge check.** If main beam must be dipped for an oncoming vehicle, can you maintain speed solely because the limit is unchanged? **Answer:** No. The limit and the safe speed under the new visibility are different constraints.

**Remember:** Adjust speed to the information the light actually gives you.

### M07-L02 · Grip is a changing resource

**Your goal:** Explain how surface conditions, tyres and driver inputs affect stability, steering and braking.

Tyres need friction to change the vehicle's speed and direction. Braking while cornering shares that capacity; ABS can help preserve steering forces during braking, but the available friction still limits the result. Water, snow and ice change the conditions at the tyre contact, so dry-road expectations cannot simply be carried into them. [VTI friction report, sections 4.2–4.3][friction]

**Requirements for ordinary light cars and their trailers, checked 1 October 2026.** Between 1 December and 31 March, winter tyres or equivalent equipment are required when winter conditions exist. Snow, ice, slush or frost on any part of the road counts; police determine conditions at the location. The minimum tread depth for the ordinary light-vehicle case is 3 mm. Studless winter tyres for the car need the mountain/snowflake marking. [Winter tyres][winter]

Studded tyres are permitted from 1 October to 15 April and outside that period when winter conditions exist or are expected. Do not mix studded and non-studded tyres on an ordinary passenger car or light goods vehicle. In winter conditions, a studded towing car requires a studded trailer; the reverse combination is allowed. A light-trailer transition permits specified winter-designed M+S tyres through 30 November 2028. Exemptions and heavier-vehicle cases need their own checks; they are not the ordinary car rule. [Winter tyres][winter] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 4 §§18a–18d][traffic-law]

For summer conditions, the minimum tread depth is 1.6 mm. Transportstyrelsen recommends placing the tyres with the best grip or greatest tread depth at the rear and checking pressure against the vehicle's instructions. A legal minimum is not a promise of equal performance in every condition. [Transportstyrelsen: tyres][tyre-basics]

**Learning approach.** Reduce speed and increase space before reaching uncertain grip. Avoid demanding abrupt braking, steering and acceleration together. Do not experiment with skid recovery in ordinary traffic. Establish the actual vehicle's emergency-braking behaviour through appropriate instruction and risk training. [Trafikverket: winter driving advice][tyre-advice] · [Risk training][risk]

**Worked decision.** Your car has suitable winter tyres, but the next bend is partly shaded and looks different from the road behind. You reduce speed before the bend and preserve margin. Equipment does not establish the grip at that particular point.

**Knowledge check.** Does a legal tread depth mean you can use the same following distance on ice as on a dry road? **Answer:** No. Adapt the gap and speed to actual conditions.

**Remember:** Tyres and assistance systems support a cautious plan; they do not create unlimited grip.

### M07-L03 · Protect your attention

**Your goal:** Recognise distraction, stress and fatigue and choose an interruption or alternative travel plan.

**Requirements.** During road travel, a driver may use communication equipment only if it does not adversely affect driving. Holding the equipment in the hand while using it is prohibited. Hands-free use is therefore not automatically acceptable: the effect on driving still matters. You must also not drive when exhaustion or another condition prevents safe control. [Transportstyrelsen: communication equipment][phone] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §1 and chapter 4 §10e][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Prepare navigation before setting off, place the phone away from casual handling and agree when passengers can help. If a conversation, screen task or emotional reaction is consuming the attention needed for the road, end it. A safe place to stop is the place to resolve a complicated route or urgent message.

Treat fatigue as a reason to change the journey. Plan rest opportunities and stop safely when concentration cannot be maintained. Do not wait for a dramatic event to prove that you are too tired. Trafikverket recommends setting out rested, allowing time and taking breaks on longer journeys. No universal break interval in this material certifies that you can continue safely. [Journey advice][planning]

**Worked decision.** A driver misses a sign during an intense hands-free conversation. The useful response is not to argue that the device is legal to use. End the conversation, reassess fitness and stop safely if attention cannot be restored.

**Knowledge check.** Is entering a destination on a mounted phone necessarily allowed because it is not hand-held? **Answer:** No. Equipment use must not adversely affect driving. Prepare it while parked or stop somewhere safe and lawful.

**Remember:** Protect attention before it is fully occupied.

### M07-L04 · Change the plan when risk grows

**Your goal:** Reassess a trip when weather, darkness, delays or passenger pressure accumulate.

**Learning approach.** At a meaningful change—worsening visibility, an unexpected closure, a missed rest opportunity—review the whole plan. Ask what has changed in the driver, vehicle, route and time available. A manageable single difficulty may become demanding when several occur together. This is a planning exercise, not a formula that assigns a numerical risk score.

Choose between continuing with a suitable margin, stopping safely, changing route, changing driver if lawful and suitable, or cancelling the journey. The choice depends on the actual conditions. A driver unable to maintain safe control must not continue merely because the destination is close. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§1 and 14][traffic-law]

The syllabus treats route choice, timing, passengers, fatigue and personal motives as parts of driving competence. Use the same questions after a journey: Which warning did I notice early? Which did I explain away because I wanted to finish? [Syllabus, chapters 4–5][syllabus]

**Worked decision.** A late appointment leaves a driver facing an unfamiliar route in darkness and worsening weather. Instead of protecting the original arrival time, the driver finds a safe stopping place, tells the person waiting and chooses a realistic alternative. The change happens while there is still time to make it calmly.

**Knowledge check.** Is deciding to cancel evidence that the original plan failed? **Answer:** It may show that reassessment worked. The aim is a safe decision under current conditions, not loyalty to an earlier plan.

**Remember:** Replanning is a driving skill.

### M07-R · Review and self-assessment

**Scenario:** You leave in daylight but meet heavy rain, darkness and a delay. A passenger wants to keep going without a break.

Explain changes to lighting, speed, space and the journey itself. **Review guide:** Check what you can see, what grip you can reasonably expect and whether you can concentrate. Do not compensate for delay by reducing margins.

Record the earliest sign that would make you change this trip. Name the consequence of ignoring it and one action you can prepare in advance. [Syllabus self-assessment, chapters 2–4 §3][syllabus]

## 4. Negotiate shared space

A safe interaction needs both a rule and an understanding of how people are moving. Establish your duty first, then keep observing. Having priority never makes a collision acceptable.

### M04-L01 · Decide who goes first

**Your goal:** Explain junction decisions using priority instructions and right-hand priority, and recognise special situations.

**Requirements.** Where two vehicle paths cross, the general rule is to yield to a vehicle approaching from your right, except where another applicable rule or instruction determines the relationship. Road width, familiarity or the fact that you are travelling straight does not by itself establish priority. Right-hand priority can apply at a three-way junction or within a parking area. [Right-hand priority][priority] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §18][traffic-law]

Different duties apply when entering a road from premises, a car park, fuel station, similar roadside area, path or similar exit, or from terrain, a pedestrian street, walking-speed area or cycle street. After crossing a footway or cycle path to enter a road, the exit provision also matters. Do not decide solely from a kerb's appearance; identify the actual layout. Joining a priority road without an acceleration lane generally requires yielding, subject to the provision for joining without changing lane. Roundabout entry has its own duty. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§21–23][traffic-law]

To yield, show your intention by reducing speed or stopping in good time. Only continue when the other road user's position, distance and speed allow movement without danger or obstruction. A stop duty requires a complete stop even if the road appears empty. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§5 and 19][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Ask three questions: What controls this conflict? Whose path crosses mine? Can I make the decision early enough that the other person understands it? Do not wave someone into a situation you cannot assess for them.

**Worked decision.** You approach from a driveway on the right of a car travelling along the road. You yield under the exit rule. Being on the other driver's right does not displace that duty.

**Knowledge check.** You drive straight on a broad road and another car approaches from the right on a narrower ordinary road. No signs, signals or special rule apply. Who yields? **Answer:** You do. The width difference alone does not replace right-hand priority.

**Remember:** Determine the applicable rule before judging the available gap.

### M04-L02 · Turn with a complete picture

**Your goal:** Plan observation, signalling, position and margins for turns and lane changes.

**Requirements.** Before a turn, check that it will not obstruct oncoming traffic or traffic on the road you are entering. Pay attention to people travelling in the same direction, including those behind you. Position early: normally the right-hand lane for a right turn and the left-hand lane for a left turn where multiple approach lanes exist, subject to applicable signs, markings and space provisions. On a two-way road, a left turn is normally prepared near the road centre; on a one-way carriageway, near the left edge. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§24–29][traffic-law]

For a lane change or significant sideways movement, signal clearly and in time, then check that the movement can be made without danger or unnecessary obstruction. The indicator announces an intention, not a claim on a gap. Drivers who notice another vehicle intending to join their lane must adjust speed to help; when two lanes merge into one, both drivers must adapt with mutual consideration. These duties work together. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§43–44 and 65–66][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Read the path through the turn, not just the point at which you steer. Check the mirror view and the areas it does not cover. Look for a cyclist alongside before turning across their route. Recheck the receiving road, crossings and queue space. If the information changes, pause the manoeuvre rather than completing the original plan automatically.

**Worked decision.** A driver signals to move right while a cyclist is beside the vehicle. The driver waits. Signalling earlier would have helped communication, but it would never have removed the need to ensure the cyclist's safety.

**Knowledge check.** Does a driver in the continuing lane have no responsibility when someone is merging? **Answer:** No. They must adapt speed to facilitate an observed entry; the joining driver must still make a safe movement.

**Remember:** Communicate the plan, then confirm the space is still there.

### M04-L03 · Crossings belong to people

**Your goal:** Distinguish pedestrian crossings, cycle passages and cycle crossings and apply the relevant duties.

**Requirements.** At an uncontrolled pedestrian crossing, yield to pedestrians who are on it or about to enter. Someone walking a bicycle is a pedestrian; someone riding one is not made a pedestrian merely by using the painted crossing. A person using a wheelchair at walking speed is covered by pedestrian rules. This distinction never removes the general duty to avoid danger. [Pedestrian crossings][pedestrians] · [Wheelchair users][wheelchairs] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 1 §4 and chapter 3 §61][traffic-law]

| Situation | Driver's duty |
|---|---|
| Uncontrolled cycle passage, approaching without turning | Adapt speed so cyclists or class II moped riders already on the passage are not endangered. A rider coming from a cycle path generally has a yielding duty. |
| Uncontrolled cycle passage after your turn or roundabout exit | Travel slowly and allow riders on or about to enter the passage an opportunity to pass. |
| Cycle crossing (*cykelöverfart*) | Yield to cyclists and class II moped riders on it or about to enter it. |

A cycle crossing has both markings and the cycle-crossing sign, together with a design that secures low vehicle speeds. Do not identify it only by painted blocks. [Transportstyrelsen: cycle passages and crossings][cycles] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§61–61a][traffic-law]

At a controlled pedestrian crossing, let people who entered correctly complete their passage even if your signal permits you to proceed. After turning across a controlled crossing, travel slowly and yield to pedestrians who have correctly entered or are about to enter. Corresponding duties apply to riders at controlled cycle passages. Keep the crossing clear when traffic ahead is queued. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§60–62][traffic-law]

**Worked decision.** You leave a roundabout and reach an uncontrolled cycle passage. A cyclist is about to enter. You slow and give the rider an opportunity to pass; applying only the straight-ahead passage rule would miss the significance of your exit.

**Knowledge check.** Is every cycle passage a cycle crossing with the same yielding duty? **Answer:** No. Identify the signs, controls and your own movement before applying the rule.

**Remember:** Expect a person to continue moving while you are interpreting the layout.

### M04-L04 · Choose a safe path through a roundabout

**Your goal:** Describe an approach, entry and exit plan that accounts for priority, lanes, signalling and nearby crossings.

**Requirements.** Travel anticlockwise and yield on entry to vehicles already circulating. Choose an appropriate lane for the onward route and obey signs and markings. There is no general requirement to signal solely for entering; signal lane changes and significant sideways movements, and signal right when leaving. Any lane change must be safe and must not create unnecessary obstruction. [Transportstyrelsen: roundabout guide][roundabout] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§13, 22, 43 and 65–66][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Read the layout before reaching the yield point. Reduce speed enough to judge circulating traffic and the available space. Observe crossings on the approach as separate conflicts. Inside, hold your intended path and continue checking nearby vehicles. A large vehicle may need more room than its apparent lane width suggests. [Roundabout guide][roundabout]

Plan the exit early. Moving from an inner lane across an outer lane is a lane change, even though you call the whole movement “leaving the roundabout.” Do not assume a vehicle beside you will leave at the same exit. If the exit cannot be taken safely, continue as the layout allows and re-plan. On leaving, check the crossing area again; the duties in M04-L03 still apply. [Roundabout guide][roundabout] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§60–62][traffic-law]

**Worked decision.** Your desired exit is approaching, but a car is alongside in the outer lane. You do not cut across it. You preserve a safe path and choose another opportunity consistent with the lane markings. Recovering the route is secondary to avoiding a conflict.

**Knowledge check.** Does a left signal give you permission to cross a neighbouring lane? **Answer:** No. Whatever the signal, a sideways movement requires a safe opportunity. Use the correct signal for the actual manoeuvre.

**Remember:** Entry, circulation, lane changes, exit and crossings are separate decisions within one journey.

### M04-R · Review and self-assessment

**Scenario:** Leave a car park, turn left, pass a cycle crossing and enter a two-lane roundabout.

State each duty before choosing a gap. **Review guide:** Apply the exit rule, check oncoming and receiving-road traffic, identify the crossing correctly, yield on roundabout entry and plan any lane change and exit without assuming priority.

Which conflict would be hardest to observe while also controlling the car? Record the consequence of noticing it late and arrange targeted supervised practice. [Syllabus self-assessment, chapter 3 §3][syllabus]

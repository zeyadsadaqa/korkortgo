## 5. Make room in busy streets

In a busy street, a small change can create a new conflict: a door opens, someone steps around a parked van, or a bus prepares to leave. Good driving leaves room for those changes.

### M05-L01 · See the person behind the movement

**Your goal:** Anticipate the needs of different road users without assuming that everyone sees, hears or moves as you do.

**Requirements.** Show particular consideration for children, older people, school patrols and people whose disability or illness is apparent and affects their use of traffic. Reduce speed appropriately near children, stopped passenger transport and other situations with foreseeable risks. Give pedestrians sufficient room and time when meeting or passing them. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 2 §1 and chapter 3 §§15 and 58][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Look for the space a person needs, not only the space they occupy now. A child may be hidden by a parked car. A cyclist may need to move out from an obstruction. A wheelchair user may need a usable kerb transition. You cannot reliably infer someone's hearing, vision or intentions from a quick glance. Allow time and observe their movement rather than demanding a response through the horn.

Horses and riders also belong in your traffic assessment. Vehicle rules apply to riding and leading horses in relevant respects; they are not simply pedestrians because they travel slowly. Give them an unhurried, spacious interaction, and avoid unnecessary noise. This is a cautious application of the general duty of care, not a fixed passing-distance rule. [Transportstyrelsen: horses in traffic][horses]

**Bus rule.** Where the maximum permitted speed is 50 km/h or lower, a driver approaching a bus whose driver signals to leave a bus stop must slow and give it the opportunity to leave, stopping if needed. The bus driver must also take particular care. If passing on the right of a bus or tram stopped at a stop without a refuge, stop to allow boarding or alighting passengers free passage. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§45 and 58][traffic-law]

**Worked decision.** On a 40 km/h street, a bus signals out. You ease speed early and leave room rather than trying to squeeze past. You also watch for people near the stop; making space for the bus is not your only task.

**Knowledge check.** Does a narrow visible gap beside a cyclist prove there is enough room to pass? **Answer:** No. Consider the rider's possible movement, roadside obstacles, oncoming traffic and a safe lateral margin. Wait if there is not enough space.

**Remember:** Predict possible needs without assuming a person will behave exactly as expected.

### M05-L02 · When the street changes purpose

**Your goal:** Distinguish ordinary streets from pedestrian streets, walking-speed areas and cycle streets.

**Requirements.** On a pedestrian street (*gågata*) and in a walking-speed area (*gångfartsområde*), drive no faster than walking pace and yield to pedestrians. The general rule prohibits parking; a specifically signed lawful parking arrangement must be assessed on its own terms. Walking pace is a condition to match, not a universal speedometer number. [Special streets][special-streets] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 8 §1 and chapter 10 §1][traffic-law]

Motor vehicles may use a pedestrian street only to cross it, apart from the specified necessary journeys: goods to or from shops or equivalent premises; goods or residents to or from addresses there; guests to or from hotels or equivalent premises; and sick people or people with mobility impairments to or from an address. A convenient shortcut does not qualify. Check additional signs and local restrictions. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 8 §1][traffic-law]

On a cycle street (*cykelgata*), the maximum is 30 km/h, and motor vehicles must adapt speed to bicycle traffic. Vehicles entering from another road yield to vehicles already on it. Parking is restricted to specially provided places. When leaving a cycle street for another road, the exit yielding rule applies. [Special streets][special-streets] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §21 and chapter 8 §1a][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** A change in street function changes your expectations. People may use more of the width; a car's arrival should not force them into hurried avoidance. Read the beginning and ending signs and identify whether your proposed journey is permitted before entering.

**Worked decision.** Your navigation suggests a pedestrian street as a shortcut. You continue to another permitted route. The navigation suggestion does not establish an exemption from the access rule.

**Knowledge check.** Is 30 km/h always appropriate on a cycle street? **Answer:** No. It is a maximum. You must adapt to the actual bicycle traffic and any lower speed needed for safety.

**Remember:** Recognise the area's purpose and adapt before crossing its boundary.

### M05-L03 · Choose a lawful place to stop

**Your goal:** Distinguish stopping from parking and interpret the rules, signs and time panels for a proposed place.

Parking is leaving a vehicle stationary for a reason other than traffic conditions, avoiding danger, boarding or alighting, or loading or unloading goods. Remaining behind the wheel does not automatically make waiting “stopping.” Nor does switching on hazard lights create permission. [Transportstyrelsen: stopping and parking][parking]

**Core requirements.** Do not stop or park where you create danger, unnecessarily obstruct traffic or hide a sign or signal. Normally use the right side in your direction of travel; one-way roads and specified rail-track situations have exceptions. Secure the vehicle against rolling and prevent unauthorised use. Open doors only without danger or unnecessary inconvenience. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§47, 50 and 52][traffic-law]

| Place | General restriction for an ordinary car |
|---|---|
| Pedestrian crossing, cycle passage or cycle crossing | No stopping on it or within 10 m before it. |
| Road junction | No stopping in it or within 10 m of the nearest edge of the crossing carriageway. |
| Crossing footway or cycle path | No stopping on it or within 10 m before it. |
| Tunnel, road underpass, hidden crest or obscured bend | No stopping in the prohibited location. |
| Beside a continuous lane boundary | No stopping if less than 3 m remains, unless a broken line lies between the car and the continuous line. |
| Railway level crossing | No stopping on it; no parking within 30 m on the road. |
| Property access | No parking in front of it or where vehicle access is substantially obstructed. |
| Priority road or signed passing place | No parking under the general rule; check any lawful signed provision. |

Other prohibitions cover cycle lanes, hatched areas, roundabouts and lanes reserved for regular public transport. These examples are not an exhaustive catalogue of local restrictions. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§47–55][traffic-law]

At a marked bus stop, other vehicles may stop only for boarding or alighting without obstructing the relevant service. If its extent is not marked, the restricted area is normally 20 m before and 5 m after the stop sign. Loading goods is not the same exception as letting a passenger get out. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §54][traffic-law]

**Read the whole parking instruction.** On a T6 time panel, ordinary black/white times without brackets mean weekdays other than the day before a Sunday or public holiday; bracketed times mean those preceding days; red times mean Sundays and public holidays. A named day applies to that day. Check the actual date, fee, duration, vehicle group and any permit condition. A disabled-parking permit has defined benefits, not unrestricted permission to stop anywhere. [Road Signs Regulation, T6–T8 and T16–T19][sign-law] · [Parking permits][parking-permit]

On municipally maintained public places within built-up areas and within public-road areas, parking is normally limited to 24 consecutive hours on weekdays other than the day before a Sunday or public holiday. This is not a blanket rule for every private car park. Where a parking disc is required, set it to the next half-hour after arrival; if you arrive before the restricted period and remain into it, set its beginning time. Display it so it can be read. Always check the applicable signs and conditions. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §49a][traffic-law]

**Worked decision.** It is an ordinary Tuesday, not a holiday or the day before one. A parking sign permits at most two hours during black unbracketed 09–18. You arrive at 10:00. The two-hour restriction applies; check the other conditions before leaving the car.

**Knowledge check.** Can you wait 8 m before a pedestrian crossing with the engine running? **Answer:** No. The general prohibition includes stopping, not just parking.

**Remember:** First check whether the place is lawful at all; then check its conditions.

### M05-L04 · Manoeuvre where people may appear

**Your goal:** Plan low-speed parking and reversing while observing changing space.

**Requirements.** Reverse or turn around only without danger or obstruction to other road users. Secure the parked vehicle and check before opening a door or letting passengers out. Parking inside a marked space requires the wheels to remain within its boundary. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§46, 49–50][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Select a place that offers both a safe entry and a safe departure. Check the full route of the vehicle: front corners can move sideways while you watch behind. Revisit both sides and the path ahead as the manoeuvre develops. Do not treat a camera, sensor or a helper's signal as proof that every part of the area is clear.

Stop when information is lost. If you need to inspect the area, first secure the vehicle and choose a safe way to do so. In supervised practice, learn the car's dimensions and the effect of steering gradually. A useful successful outcome is not “I parked in one movement”; it is “I kept control and did not move into uncertain space.”

**Worked decision.** A delivery vehicle blocks the view of the footway behind your parking place. Rather than reversing across it in one continuous movement, you reassess the layout with your supervisor and choose a method or another place that provides the necessary view. If a pedestrian enters the path, you wait.

**Knowledge check.** Is a pedestrian obliged to avoid your car because you began reversing first? **Answer:** No. Your reversing duty remains; stop before creating a conflict.

**Remember:** A small adjustment or a pause can be evidence of good judgement.

### M05-R · Review and self-assessment

**Scenario:** You reach a busy neighbourhood to collect a passenger. A bus is signalling out, a cyclist is beside parked cars and the nearest space is before a crossing.

Explain how you will approach and choose a lawful collection point. **Review guide:** Account for the bus duty where applicable, the cyclist's space, the crossing restriction and safe boarding. Convenience does not create a parking exemption.

Record one observation that was difficult to make early, and plan a supervised exercise to improve it. [Syllabus self-assessment, chapter 3 §3][syllabus]

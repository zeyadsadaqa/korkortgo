## 10. Respond, reflect and keep learning

When a journey is interrupted, the setting determines the safest response. Afterwards, use what happened to identify a specific learning need rather than simply deciding that you were lucky or unlucky.

### M10-L01 · When the car cannot continue

**Your goal:** Compare a breakdown on an ordinary road, a motorway and in a tunnel.

**Requirement.** If a breakdown leaves a vehicle where stopping is prohibited, it must be moved to a suitable place as soon as possible, subject to the special rules for an accident scene. Warning obligations depend on the situation. A warning triangle must give approaching drivers enough warning; the law does not prescribe one universal distance for every road. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§51 and 57][traffic-law]

**Official motorway/2+1-road advice.** If possible, reach the safest available stopping place. Switch on hazard lights, warn other traffic and leave the vehicle and carriageway promptly. Move away from traffic, outside a barrier where one exists. Trafikverket recommends placing the triangle at least 100 m away, preferably farther, and taking care while doing so, walking at the side and outside the barrier where possible. Do not expose yourself in the traffic lane to carry out a roadside repair. Call professional recovery; call 112 when life is in danger. [Trafikverket: breakdown advice][breakdown]

**Tunnel advice is different.** Use hazard lights, move towards the edge or an emergency bay if possible and switch off the engine. For a non-urgent incident, Trafikverket advises staying in the car or protected from traffic close to it while calling for assistance. In an urgent situation, leave for the nearest emergency exit and use the assistance point, emergency phone or 112. In a fire, leave and reach an emergency exit; follow signs, announcements and emergency personnel. Do not turn a non-urgent-tunnel recommendation into advice to stay beside a fire. [Trafikverket: tunnels][tunnel]

**Learning approach.** On an ordinary road, assess traffic, visibility and the location before choosing a protected waiting place and arranging help. Do not assume that a procedure for a sheltered bay, an exposed motorway shoulder and a smoke-filled tunnel can be identical.

**Worked decision.** A driver with a puncture on a motorway wants to change the wheel immediately. They first protect people from traffic and arrange recovery rather than working in an exposed position. A warning triangle does not create a protected workshop.

**Knowledge check.** Is “always stay in the car” or “always leave in exactly the same way” a complete rule for every breakdown? **Answer:** Neither. Apply the official guidance for the location and urgency, with people’s safety first.

**Remember:** Establish where the danger is before deciding where to wait.

### M10-L02 · When someone may be hurt

**Your goal:** Identify scene-safety, emergency-contact and first-aid learning needs following a collision.

**Requirements.** If involved in a traffic accident, remain at the scene, help injured people to the extent of your ability and assist with necessary measures. Provide your name, address and information when requested by another involved person or someone whose property was damaged. Notify the owner or police promptly if damaged property has nobody present to receive the information. Notify police promptly for a personal injury that is not insignificant. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 2 §8][traffic-law]

A vehicle creating danger or obstruction after a collision should be moved to a suitable place. However, where someone has died or been seriously injured, move it only if it endangers other traffic, and preserve evidence as required. Staying at the scene does not mean standing in an active traffic lane. Protect yourself and others while arranging help. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 2 §8][traffic-law]

**Emergency response.** At danger to life, call 112. Give the location, direction of travel, what happened and what dangers you can see; follow the operator's instructions. Make observations from a safe position rather than walking into moving traffic to gather details. [Trafikverket: emergency contact][breakdown]

**First-aid reference.** Use [1177's first-aid guidance][first-aid] to learn more about helping someone in an acute situation, including when a person is unconscious or has breathing difficulties. Practical first-aid training can help you prepare. This lesson is deliberately brief and does not reproduce clinical procedures or a pulse-check sequence. In an emergency, call 112 rather than spending time searching a website.

**Wildlife collisions.** Trafikverket advises calling 112 and marking the collision location safely so responders can find it, even if the animal has left. Avoid approaching an injured animal. Its guidance gives a separate precaution for wild boar, bear, lynx and wolf: continue 200 m before stopping and marking, then call police immediately. Only move the vehicle when safe and possible; explain the actual collision location and the animal's direction clearly. This is agency safety guidance, not a claim that every animal species has identical statutory reporting rules. [Trafikverket: wildlife collisions][wildlife]

**Worked decision.** After a collision, a person is badly injured and vehicles are still passing. You call 112 and describe the immediate traffic danger from a protected position, following instructions and helping within your ability. Photographing damage is not the first priority.

**Knowledge check.** Should you leave a collision you were involved in simply because you believe you were not at fault? **Answer:** No. The duty to remain and assist applies regardless of fault.

**Remember:** Protect people, summon help and cooperate. Learn detailed first aid from the dedicated healthcare guidance and training.

### M10-L03 · Explain a whole journey

**Your goal:** Review a mixed journey, justify decisions, identify missed hazards and propose safer alternatives.

This exercise brings the earlier lessons together. It is an original learning scenario, not an official test question or a court case.

**Scenario.** You plan to take a passenger to a rural appointment using an unfamiliar car. The outward journey includes a residential area, a roundabout and a faster road. Rain is expected later. At the destination, the nearest empty space is before a pedestrian crossing. On the return journey you become delayed; the passenger asks you to pass a slower vehicle near a bend.

**Work through the decisions.**

1. Before leaving, establish fitness, entitlement, vehicle readiness, passenger protection and the controls you may need. Check the route and alternatives.
2. In the residential area, identify crossings, people hidden by parked vehicles and the applicable speed. At the roundabout, separate entry, lane and exit decisions.
3. On the faster section, preserve a following gap and a view ahead. If the weather worsens, reassess both lighting and speed.
4. At the destination, apply the stopping restrictions before considering convenience. Choose a lawful place with a safe manoeuvre and passenger exit.
5. On the return trip, reject an overtaking attempt without a complete clear path. Change the arrival expectation instead of the safety margin.

The rules behind these decisions are explained in M02–M07. The duties to adapt speed, yield where required and avoid dangerous manoeuvres remain applicable throughout. [Traffic Regulation, chapters 2–3][traffic-law]

**Worked reflection.** “I noticed the bend but focused on the passenger's request. Next time I will state that I am waiting before I start looking for a gap.” This identifies a trigger and an action. “I must be more careful” does not explain what to practise.

**Knowledge check.** If no collision occurred, does that prove every decision was sound? **Answer:** No. Review whether the decision depended on luck, another person avoiding you, or a margin you could not justify.

**Remember:** Assess the decision process as well as the outcome.

### M10-L04 · Make a realistic readiness plan

**Your goal:** Compare knowledge, practical performance and confidence, then plan further learning and accessible examination preparation.

The official theory test covers vehicle knowledge/manoeuvring, environment, road safety, traffic rules and personal factors. It checks understanding across those areas; memorising the wording of practice questions is not equivalent preparation. The practical test requires you to apply knowledge through vehicle checks, manoeuvres and driving in traffic. [Trafikverket: category B tests][test] · [Practical test][practical]

**Current standard theory-test format, checked 1 October 2026:** 70 questions, of which 65 count towards the score; at least 52 points are needed to pass; standard time is 50 minutes. These administrative figures can change, so verify them before booking. They are not a recommended pass mark for this book's short learning checks. [Trafikverket: theory-test format][test]

Trafikverket provides language options, including English, and application routes for support such as extended time, oral support or an interpreter where appropriate. Support generally needs approval before the supported test can be booked. Check the current requirements and apply early enough for a decision; do not assume that support can be added at the door. [Languages][languages] · [Test support][support]

**Learning approach.** Review three kinds of evidence: Can you explain the rule and its exceptions? Can you recognise the situation without a prompt? Can you act safely and consistently during supervised practice? A high confidence rating with little evidence is a learning signal, not a readiness certificate.

Write your next practice plan in concrete terms: “Recognise cycle passages after a turn and explain the duty before reaching them,” or “Demonstrate the safety check and explain each defect response.” Revisit errors after a delay and in a changed scenario so that you are practising understanding rather than recognising an answer.

**Worked decision.** A learner answers most checks correctly but still needs reminders to scan at junctions. They continue targeted supervised practice instead of treating their theory score as proof of practical readiness.

**Knowledge check.** Can this manuscript or its checks certify that you will pass or are ready to drive alone? **Answer:** No. Use it to guide learning alongside official requirements, qualified instruction and demonstrated practical competence.

**Remember:** Readiness is supported by evidence across knowledge, judgement and practical performance.

### M10-R · Review and self-assessment

**Scenario:** Review an entire journey that included an unexpected stop and a decision you would now change.

Separate what you knew, what you noticed and what you did. **Review guide:** Identify the relevant duty, the information available at the time, a safer alternative and one practical learning action. Refer back to the appropriate module and official source.

Write a dated plan for further study or supervised practice, including how you will know that the skill has improved. Reassess again after practising; completing the book is the beginning of a continuing habit. [Syllabus self-assessment, chapters 2–5 §3][syllabus]

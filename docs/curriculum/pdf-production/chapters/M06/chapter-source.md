## 6. Travel beyond the town

Higher speeds reduce the time available between seeing a problem and reaching it. On rural roads and faster routes, plan further ahead and keep enough room to change your mind.

### M06-L01 · Read the rural road

**Your goal:** Identify risks from bends, crests, narrow roads, animals and slow vehicles and justify speed and position changes.

**Requirements.** Keep right when meeting another vehicle and leave a safe lateral gap. If an obstruction on your side prevents a safe meeting, stop if necessary to let the approaching vehicle pass. Your speed must suit the visible road and foreseeable hazards, with particular care at bends, crests and narrow meetings. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§14–15 and 30][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Read the road in sections: the visible straight, the entry to a bend, the portion hidden behind a crest and the next place someone might join. Never extend the clear section in your imagination. A vehicle emerging from a field or a cyclist beyond a bend changes what seemed to be an empty road.

Wild animals and reindeer can appear throughout the year. Trafikverket highlights dawn and dusk and periods of increased animal movement, but the absence of those conditions is not a guarantee. A wildlife fence can be interrupted by access points and does not remove the need to observe. [Trafikverket: wildlife][wildlife]

A slow vehicle's appearance may resemble a car even when its permitted speed and construction differ. Trafikverket advises other drivers to keep distance and pass only where appropriate. Stay far enough back to see around it and evaluate the road, rather than moving close to demand that it hurry. [Slow vehicles and other traffic][slow-vehicles]

**Worked decision.** You approach a narrow bridge with a van coming towards you and an obstruction on your side. You reduce speed before the narrowing and stop if necessary. Do not move into the van's path simply because you reached the obstruction first.

**Knowledge check.** You can see no vehicle on the far side of a crest. Does that prove the road is clear there? **Answer:** No. It proves only that the view is limited. Choose speed and position for that uncertainty.

**Remember:** Let what you can actually see determine the available space.

### M06-L02 · Is passing worth the risk?

**Your goal:** Compare waiting with overtaking using visibility, speed difference, space and a safe completion path.

**Requirements.** Overtake only without danger and with sufficient clear road. Do not start when a following driver has begun overtaking, or pass on the left when the vehicle ahead signals an intended leftward movement. Leave safe lateral clearance and a safe return opportunity. When being overtaken, do not increase speed or otherwise make the pass harder. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§31–38][traffic-law]

The normal passing side is left. Exceptions include a vehicle turning or clearly preparing to turn left, and the specified rules for marked same-direction lanes on roads limited to 70 km/h or less. Passing in queues or destination lanes has separate provisions; it does not remove the need to protect people at crossings. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§7, 31 and 41–42][traffic-law]

**Restrictions to recognise.** Overtaking is generally prohibited immediately before or in a road junction, with specified exceptions for right-side overtaking, police/signal control, or an established yielding/stopping duty on the crossing road. Similar restrictions apply at level crossings, with exceptions for barriers or road-junction-type traffic signals. At an obscured bend or crest, do not use an opposing lane to overtake; the detailed provision contains limited exceptions. Section 39 also contains an exception for passing two-wheeled vehicles without sidecars. That exception does not override the danger prohibition, continuous-line restrictions or the uncontrolled-crossing prohibition. None of these exceptions proves a manoeuvre safe. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §39][traffic-law]

Do not overtake immediately before or on an uncontrolled pedestrian crossing, uncontrolled cycle passage or cycle crossing. Where parallel-lane passing is legally not classified as overtaking, the special crossing rule still requires a speed allowing an immediate stop for a person on the crossing or passage. Obey signs and continuous-line restrictions as well. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§11, 40–41][traffic-law]

**Learning approach.** Before moving, identify the full pass and return path, not merely the first gap. If completing it depends on another driver braking or on exceeding the limit, decline it. Small apparent time savings are not a reason to consume every margin. [Trafikverket: speed and journey time][time]

**Worked decision.** A vehicle ahead travels slightly slower than you want. An approaching bend hides the final part of the pass. You stay behind at a useful distance. The first few clear metres do not establish a complete overtaking opportunity.

**Knowledge check.** If the vehicle ahead accelerates during your pass, must you complete it at any cost? **Answer:** No. Reassess and take the safest available action; do not treat finishing as more important than avoiding a collision. Plan with enough margin that abandoning is possible before committing.

**Remember:** Choosing not to pass is a complete decision.

### M06-L03 · Join faster traffic calmly

**Your goal:** Plan motorway entry, lane choice, separation and exit, including a missed exit.

**Requirements.** Motorways are for eligible motor vehicles and combinations designed and permitted to travel at least 40 km/h; class I mopeds are excluded. This is an eligibility condition, not a command to maintain 40 km/h in a queue. Enter and leave through the designated starts, ends and access routes. Do not reverse, turn around, use the central divider or cross-connections, or stop outside designated parking/rest areas except as an unavoidable incident requires. The relevant restrictions also apply to expressways (*motortrafikled*) and their access routes. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 9 §§1–2][traffic-law]

In an acceleration lane, adapt speed to the traffic in the lane you intend to join and enter as soon as it can be done without danger or unnecessary obstruction. A safe joining movement is your responsibility; other drivers who notice the intended entry must adapt speed to facilitate it. Entry without an acceleration lane has a yielding rule. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§21, 23 and 43][traffic-law]

Normally use the rightmost lane intended for your vehicle. Keep a following distance suited to speed, visibility and conditions; do not copy a car ahead that is following too closely. Signs may establish a lower maximum than a road's general limit, and your vehicle combination may have a lower limit again. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§2 and 7, chapter 4 §20 and chapter 9 §1][traffic-law]

For an exit, take the suitable lane in good time and enter the deceleration lane as soon as possible. If you miss the exit, continue to a permitted exit and re-plan; never reverse to recover it. A breakdown is dealt with in M10-L01, not by using the shoulder as a convenient place to check navigation.

**Worked decision.** Your exit sign appears while another vehicle occupies the lane you would need to cross. You do not force the move. You continue safely and tell your passenger the route will change.

**Knowledge check.** Does a posted motorway limit override the lower permitted speed of your trailer combination? **Answer:** No. Both road and vehicle limits apply; use the lower applicable limit and reduce further when conditions require it.

**Remember:** Prepare the next movement before the road makes it urgent.

### M06-L04 · Expect an interrupted route

**Your goal:** Interpret railway-crossing and roadworks information and approach changed lanes, queues and barriers safely.

**Railway requirements.** Approach with particular care and at a speed that allows a stop before the crossing. Do not enter when a train or tram is approaching, a red signal shows, an audible warning sounds, a barrier is lowering, lowered or rising, or you might have to stop on the tracks. Cross without unnecessary delay only when it is permitted and there is room beyond. [Traffic Regulation, chapter 2 §7][traffic-law]

**Two different emergencies.** Trafikverket says that if warnings begin while you are already crossing, continue out. If a functioning vehicle is trapped between the barriers, drive through the barrier to leave the tracks. If the vehicle has broken down on the tracks, evacuate everyone promptly, move well away and call 112. Give the crossing ID from the crossbuck sign if available without returning to danger. Do not stay on the tracks attempting a repair or pushing exercise. [Trafikverket: level-crossing safety][rail]

**Roadworks.** Expect changed lanes, reduced speeds, workers and queues. Follow the current signs, signals and authorised guard directions; allow room around work areas and keep distance. A familiar road may now have a different usable path. Temporary guidance deserves attention before you reach the first barrier, not after you have entered the work area. [Trafikverket: roadworks][works] · [Traffic Regulation, chapter 2 §2 and chapter 3 §15][traffic-law]

**Worked decision.** Traffic beyond a railway crossing stops while the barriers are still raised. You wait before the crossing. An open barrier is not proof that there is space to get clear. Later, at roadworks, you read the temporary lane instructions instead of following the old route from memory.

**Knowledge check.** Your engine fails while the car is on the tracks. Is the priority to protect the car or to evacuate and call for help? **Answer:** Evacuate people, get away from the railway and call 112. A disabled vehicle and a vehicle still able to drive through a barrier require different responses.

**Remember:** Never enter a space unless you can clear it, and protect people first when the situation changes.

### M06-R · Review and self-assessment

**Scenario:** Your rural trip involves a slow vehicle, a motorway entry, temporary roadworks and a railway crossing with a queue beyond it.

Identify where you might have to wait or change the route. **Review guide:** A complete overtaking path, a safe entry gap, current temporary instructions and clear space beyond the tracks matter more than maintaining progress.

Record one situation in which impatience could change your judgement. Explain the risk and identify a practice or planning action that preserves your margin. [Syllabus self-assessment, chapter 3 §3][syllabus]

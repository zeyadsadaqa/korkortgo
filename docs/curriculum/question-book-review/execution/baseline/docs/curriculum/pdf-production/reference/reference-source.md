## Appendix A · Put the learning into practice

These are original study checklists. Use the cited lessons for the detailed rules and limitations; a tick does not certify a vehicle or driver as safe.

### Before a journey

- Establish that the driver is legally permitted and actually fit to drive. Resolve uncertainty about fatigue, medication or conditions before leaving. See M01-L01/L02.
- Check the route, weather, traffic, realistic timing, stopping opportunities and a fallback option. Include the return journey. See M01-L04 and M07-L04.
- Check the vehicle, usable visibility, required lighting and controls. Confirm protection for each passenger. See M02.
- Establish load and trailer limits, coupling and securing if relevant. Verify the vehicle's recorded status and obligations. See M08.
- Prepare navigation and passenger help before moving. Keep attention available for traffic. See M07-L03.

### While approaching a new situation

1. **Observe:** What can I see, what is hidden and who might enter?
2. **Identify the duty:** Which rule, sign, marking, signal or authorised instruction applies?
3. **Preserve space:** Can I stop or alter the plan without forcing another person to avoid me?
4. **Communicate:** Will others understand my intended movement?
5. **Recheck:** Is the information still true as I begin and complete the action?

This is an original learning routine based on the observation, risk and cooperation aims of the [category B syllabus][syllabus]. It is not an official test sequence.

### Before leaving a parked vehicle

Check that stopping and parking are permitted at that location, time and for that vehicle. Meet fee, duration and permit conditions. Secure against rolling and unauthorised use. Check for people before opening doors and provide a safe place for passengers to leave. See M05-L03/L04 and the [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§47–55][traffic-law].

A printed still image alone cannot demonstrate flashing, changing, audible or hand-movement signals; learn the sequence as well as the appearance.

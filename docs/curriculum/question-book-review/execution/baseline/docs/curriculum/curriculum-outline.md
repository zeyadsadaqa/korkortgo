# Original Curriculum Outline

**Version:** 1.0 · **Prepared:** 1 October 2026 · **Status:** Approved by the user on 1 October 2026

This proposed category B learning structure follows decisions a learner makes before, during and after a journey. Its 10 modules contain 40 lessons and 10 module self-assessments. Titles, objectives and scenario briefs are original. The [official-source map](curriculum-source-map.md) records support and remaining research for every objective. Planning IDs below are review references; final app identifiers and question migration belong to step 7.

## Required opening notice

> This material is a learning aid to help you understand Swedish driving theory. It is not an official source and cannot replace official information from Trafikverket and Transportstyrelsen. Always consult their current guidance and the applicable rules.

Place this prominently in the book introduction, before the first teaching module. Explain how to find the official references and their verification dates. Practical skills require supervised practice; reading and self-assessment alone cannot demonstrate competence.

## Sequence and lesson approach

Study modules 1–3 first to establish readiness, vehicle preparation and road interpretation. Continue through modules 4–7 to apply those foundations in increasingly complex situations. Modules 8–9 extend vehicle responsibility and travel choices; module 10 integrates the whole curriculum. Revisit personal readiness, environmental impact and self-assessment throughout, rather than leaving them until the final review.

Each future lesson should contain an everyday situation, an original explanation, a clearly distinguished rule or recommendation, a worked decision, a short knowledge check with reasoning, and official references. Scenario briefs below are authoring prompts, not a completed manuscript or assessed practical tasks. Do not assign official examination weightings or promise a pass based on these checks.

## 1. Choose to drive responsibly

**Entry point:** Start here; no previous module required.

| Lesson | Learning objective |
|---|---|
| M01-L01 · Your route into driving | Build a personal preparation checklist covering entitlement, permits, supervised practice, risk training and examinations. |
| M01-L02 · Am I ready today? | Decide whether to drive in scenarios involving tiredness, illness, medication, alcohol or other drugs, and explain a safer alternative. |
| M01-L03 · Pressure is a driving risk | Identify how peers, impulses, confidence and personal circumstances can change a driving decision; rehearse a refusal or alternative. |
| M01-L04 · Plan the journey before the ignition | Compare route, departure time, transport mode and passenger choices for safety, accessibility and environmental impact. |

**Module scenario:** Plan a first week of learning, then decide whether to proceed with a late journey under social pressure.

**M01-R — Self-assessment:** After the module scenario, identify one uncertain decision, explain its possible consequences, and choose an official reference or supervised practice activity to address it.

## 2. Prepare a car that is ready

**Entry point:** Complete the preceding modules.

| Lesson | Learning objective |
|---|---|
| M02-L01 · Know what the controls tell you | Explain the purpose of core controls, warning indicators, braking and steering systems, and identify when further instructions are needed. |
| M02-L02 · A check before moving | Organise a safety check of tyres, lights, visibility and vehicle condition, and distinguish a defect from a reason to seek help. |
| M02-L03 · Protect everyone inside | Select appropriate restraint arrangements in adult and child passenger scenarios and explain how belts, headrests and airbags work together. |
| M02-L04 · Move with control | Plan observation and control for starting, stopping, reversing, hill starts and turning; explain the need for supervised practical practice. |

**Module scenario:** Prepare an unfamiliar car for a journey with passengers and explain which checks need assistance.

**M02-R — Self-assessment:** After the module scenario, identify one uncertain decision, explain its possible consequences, and choose an official reference or supervised practice activity to address it.

## 3. Read the road before acting

**Entry point:** Complete the preceding modules.

| Lesson | Learning objective |
|---|---|
| M03-L01 · Recognise what a sign asks | Classify warning, priority, prohibition, mandatory and instruction signs and describe the decision each category prompts. |
| M03-L02 · Combine the instructions | Interpret a sign with a supplementary panel, road marking or signal and identify when a police or guard instruction must be considered. |
| M03-L03 · Find your way without rushing | Use direction signs, symbols, facility and service signs to plan a route choice early, including walking and cycling destinations. |
| M03-L04 · Leave room to respond | Explain how observation, speed, reaction time, braking, grip and following space affect the options available in a developing hazard. |

**Module scenario:** Follow a signed route where markings, a signal and a supplementary panel change the next decision.

**M03-R — Self-assessment:** After the module scenario, identify one uncertain decision, explain its possible consequences, and choose an official reference or supervised practice activity to address it.

## 4. Negotiate shared space

**Entry point:** Complete modules 1–3 first.

| Lesson | Learning objective |
|---|---|
| M04-L01 · Decide who goes first | Explain a junction decision using priority signs and right-hand priority, and recognise situations requiring further rule checks. |
| M04-L02 · Turn with a complete picture | Plan observation, signalling, position and margins for turns and lane changes, accounting for blind spots and other road users. |
| M04-L03 · Crossings belong to people | Distinguish pedestrian crossings, cycle passages and cycle crossings and explain the driver's responsibilities in a selected scenario. |
| M04-L04 · Choose a safe path through a roundabout | Describe an approach, entry and exit plan that accounts for priority, lanes, signalling and people crossing nearby. |

**Module scenario:** Explain each decision through a junction, a cycle crossing and a roundabout.

**M04-R — Self-assessment:** After the module scenario, identify one uncertain decision, explain its possible consequences, and choose an official reference or supervised practice activity to address it.

## 5. Make room in busy streets

**Entry point:** Complete modules 1–3 first.

| Lesson | Learning objective |
|---|---|
| M05-L01 · See the person behind the movement | Anticipate the needs of children, older people, wheelchair users, cyclists, riders and motorcyclists in street scenarios. |
| M05-L02 · When the street changes purpose | Compare ordinary streets with walking-speed areas, pedestrian streets and cycle streets and choose a suitable driving response. |
| M05-L03 · Choose a lawful place to stop | Distinguish stopping from parking and interpret signs and time panels for a proposed parking place. |
| M05-L04 · Manoeuvre where people may appear | Plan a low-speed parking or reversing manoeuvre with observation, space checks and a decision to stop when the view is insufficient. |

**Module scenario:** Choose where to stop in a busy neighbourhood and plan a manoeuvre around people with limited visibility.

**M05-R — Self-assessment:** After the module scenario, identify one uncertain decision, explain its possible consequences, and choose an official reference or supervised practice activity to address it.

## 6. Travel beyond the town

**Entry point:** Complete modules 1–3 first.

| Lesson | Learning objective |
|---|---|
| M06-L01 · Read the rural road | Identify risks from bends, crests, narrow roads, animals and slow vehicles and justify changes in speed and position. |
| M06-L02 · Is passing worth the risk? | Compare staying behind with overtaking in a scenario using visibility, closing speed, space and the ability to abandon the attempt. |
| M06-L03 · Join faster traffic calmly | Plan a motorway entry, lane choice, separation and exit, including a safe response to a missed exit. |
| M06-L04 · Expect an interrupted route | Interpret railway-crossing and roadworks information and plan a safe approach to changed lanes, queues or a blocked crossing. |

**Module scenario:** Plan a rural journey that joins a motorway and encounters roadworks and a railway crossing.

**M06-R — Self-assessment:** After the module scenario, identify one uncertain decision, explain its possible consequences, and choose an official reference or supervised practice activity to address it.

## 7. Adapt when conditions change

**Entry point:** Complete modules 1–3 first.

| Lesson | Learning objective |
|---|---|
| M07-L01 · Drive within what you can see | Choose a visibility and lighting strategy for darkness, fog and glare, considering both seeing and being seen. |
| M07-L02 · Grip is a changing resource | Explain how rain, standing water, ice, snow, tyres and driver inputs can affect stability, steering and braking. |
| M07-L03 · Protect your attention | Recognise distraction, stress and fatigue in a journey scenario and choose a practical interruption or alternative travel plan. |
| M07-L04 · Change the plan when risk grows | Reassess a trip when weather, darkness, delays or passenger pressure accumulate and justify continuing, pausing or cancelling. |

**Module scenario:** Reassess an evening trip as rain, reduced visibility and fatigue develop.

**M07-R — Self-assessment:** After the module scenario, identify one uncertain decision, explain its possible consequences, and choose an official reference or supervised practice activity to address it.

## 9. Reduce the impact of each journey

**Entry point:** Complete modules 1–3 first.

|---|---|

## 10. Respond, reflect and keep learning

**Entry point:** Complete the preceding modules.

| Lesson | Learning objective |
|---|---|
| M10-L01 · When the car cannot continue | Compare the response to a breakdown on an ordinary road, a motorway and in a tunnel, explaining how the location changes the priorities. |
| M10-L02 · When someone may be hurt | Identify scene-safety, emergency contact and first-aid learning needs following a collision, and distinguish knowledge from hands-on training. |
| M10-L03 · Explain a whole journey | Review a mixed urban and rural scenario, justify decisions, identify missed hazards and propose safer alternatives. |
| M10-L04 · Make a realistic readiness plan | Compare knowledge, practical performance and confidence, then create a plan for further supervised practice and accessible examination preparation. |

**Module scenario:** Review a complete journey with an interruption, distinguish safe immediate actions from procedures needing training, and write a practice plan.

**M10-R — Self-assessment:** After the module scenario, identify one uncertain decision, explain its possible consequences, and choose an official reference or supervised practice activity to address it.

## Reference material planned for the book

- An official-sign reference organised by purpose, with lesson cross-links: warning, priority, prohibition, mandatory and instruction signs; directions, facilities, services and tourism; information, symbols and supplementary panels; markings, signals, police and guard instructions; other devices and railway-crossing signs. Asset selection, variants, labels, reuse and print quality are step 4 work.
- A glossary explaining Swedish terms alongside clear English learning language, without inventing official English legal terminology.
- A journey-preparation checklist, vehicle-responsibility checklist and personal practice record drawn from the approved lessons.
- An official-sources section with verification dates and links, plus a clear distinction between binding requirements, official advice and original teaching examples.

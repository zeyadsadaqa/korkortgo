### Proposed format and supplied input

Propose one **unscored, text-based checklist exercise with an explained review**, using the existing practice/reflection approach. This is a content specification, not a new screen design. It requires neither artwork nor personal data entry. Do not force the whole objective into a single best-answer MCQ.

A future author supplies a fictional dossier with a fixed reference date, age, residence/study status, EEA entitlement status, intended vehicle/transmission, permit/condition/expiry records, private-supervisor approval status, valid identification, risk-part records, theory-pass date/result expiry and any approved extension. Every required fact is either explicit or deliberately marked unknown. Include two short theory-date comparison records on opposite sides of 19 August; do not require learners to infer their own legal status or calculate ambiguous calendar expiry dates.

The learner returns a checklist with columns **requirement / evidence or missing fact / next action / needed before which stage**, followed by a short explanation of whether the proposed private practice and planned tests can proceed. Unknown facts require verification and a named agency/record, not an invented approval.

### Authoring cases and plausible mistakes

| Case to cover in the future dossier | Required reasoning | Plausible mistake to diagnose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private practice after 1 August; permit valid but supervisor application pending | Wait for approval for this learner; abolished course does not remove supervision | Relative's experience or an application alone treated as approval |
| Private practice possible; only risk part 1 completed | Distinguish practice permission from test readiness; complete valid part 2 before tests | Course abolition treated as risk-training abolition, or both parts demanded before any practice |
| Two theory records, one before and one from 19 August | Use applicable transition and actual result/extension record | Automatically grant the earlier pass a year, or apply four months to both |
| Ordinary first-licence route versus an existing EEA entitlement/condition flagged for checking | Identify the route/condition needing official confirmation | Treat every learner as an unrestricted first-time applicant |
| Missing/expiring record and an attractive early test slot | Resolve the record and plan practice around skills and valid prerequisites | Guess an expiry, or treat a booking as proof of readiness |

Keep vehicle weights/trailer calculations outside this exercise; use the vehicle type only to select the applicable practice age. Do not present administrative permission as proof of day-to-day fitness to drive.

## M07-L04 · Change the plan when risk grows — 9N2

**Status:** Brief complete, 19 October 2026; proposed content requires a separate content decision before authoring or integration.

### Input and objective

Use the [M07-L04 objective and teaching](../manuscript.md#m07-l04--change-the-plan-when-risk-grows), C058 in the [evidence register](../manuscript-evidence.md#c058--m07-l04), and the complete [9B17 import](implementation-evidence/9B17/README.md). The [7D gap carried into the backlog](implementation-backlog.md#9n--close-assessment-coverage-briefs-without-changing-the-baseline-bank) records **zero primary and one related question**. In the [question map](question-map.json), `situation-053` is primarily M01-L04 and related to M07-L04; retain that assignment. Related coverage does not demonstrate a learner reassessing an evolving journey. The existing cancellation knowledge check alone does not demonstrate that skill either.

Assess whether the learner can reassess a trip as weather, darkness, delays and passenger pressure accumulate, explain how the combination reduces available margins, and choose a justified adaptation, safe stop or route/journey change. The learner must distinguish a manageable change from circumstances in which continued driving is unsafe, without a numerical risk formula.

### Sources and scope rechecked 19 October 2026

| Source | Basis for the proposed assessment |
| --- | --- |
| [Traffic Regulation, chapter 3 §§1 and 14](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/trafikforordning-19981276_sfs-1998-1276/) | Inability to drive safely, including from fatigue, prevents continued driving. Speed must suit conditions and preserve control and stopping within the visible road before foreseeable obstacles. These are constraints, not permission to continue whenever speed is reduced. |
| [Category B syllabus, TSFS 2011:20, chapters 4–5 §§2–3](https://www.transportstyrelsen.se/TSFS/TSFS%202011_20.pdf) | Journey choices, conditions, stress/fatigue, personal motives and passenger influence support reasoned planning and self-assessment. The exercise and rubric below are original teaching proposals, not an agency-prescribed test. |

These checks support this brief only; frozen teaching, release source dates and legal classifications remain intact. Future authors must recheck the cited provisions. Do not introduce weather thresholds, fixed safe speeds, fatigue recovery times or numerical risk totals unsupported by this lesson.

### Proposed format, supplied input and learner output

Propose one **unscored, staged text scenario with explained review**, using existing practice/reflection conventions. This specifies content only, without proposing a screen or artwork. A future author supplies an initial journey plan and three successive decision points. Each point states driver fitness, vehicle readiness, visibility/weather, route information, remaining time, passenger demands and available safe stopping/alternative options. Changed facts must be explicit; unknown route information must be labelled unknown. Keep the driver and vehicle initially fit and suitable so the first decision can assess adaptation rather than automatic cancellation.

The learner produces a short decision record at each point: **what changed / how the risks interact / chosen action and immediate safe execution / rejected alternative and reason / next reassessment trigger**. Finish with a brief reflection on which warning prompted replanning and how arrival pressure was handled. Decisions use only information available at that point; later facts must not retrospectively make a defensible earlier answer wrong.

### Required authoring cases and plausible mistakes

| Decision point | Facts the author must supply | Required reasoning and mistake to diagnose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adapt while margins remain | Darkness and rain develop; driver remains alert, vehicle suitable, and observation/control remain possible with changed speed and spacing | Justify an appropriate adaptation and monitoring trigger. Diagnose treating the posted limit or original arrival time as a target. Do not make cancellation the only acceptable answer in this case. |
| Stop to reassess and choose a route | A closure adds delay; passenger urges an unfamiliar shortcut; a safe legal stopping place is available before the decision, and the alternative route's suitability is unknown | Stop safely to check options and revise timing. A route change needs evidence of suitability; neither a shorter route nor staying on the original route is automatically safer. Diagnose changing navigation while driving or accepting passenger urgency as route evidence. |
| End this driver's continuation | New facts explicitly establish that the driver cannot remain alert/control the vehicle safely; the vehicle is already at a safe stopping place | Do not resume with this driver in that state. Consider a lawful, fit replacement driver only if supplied, or alternative transport/postponement/cancellation. Diagnose “nearly there”, coffee, reduced speed or a route change being used to excuse unresolved inability. |

The second point must retain the first point's darkness/weather and add delay/pressure, so the task tests accumulation rather than three unrelated hazards. The final point begins safely stopped; it must not require continuing to a distant rest area despite inability to drive safely. Any alternative driver must have explicit suitable entitlement, fitness and availability. A proposed route is checked while stopped; if its suitability remains unknown, the learner may reject it and choose another feasible safe alternative. Include at least one feasible fallback in every decision point.

### Checkable acceptance rubric

Mark each row **met / needs revision**, citing the decision record. All six must be met to demonstrate the objective. These are review criteria, not an app score or an official pass threshold.

| Criterion | Observable evidence needed for “met” |
| --- | --- |
| Detect change | Identifies the new facts at every point and carries forward relevant earlier conditions; revisits driver, vehicle, route and time rather than treating the plan as fixed. |
| Explain combined risk | Connects at least two concurrent factors to a concrete loss of margin, such as darkness/rain reducing observation while delay and passenger demands encourage rushing; a hazard list alone is insufficient. |
| Adapt proportionately | At the manageable first point, gives a defensible adaptation and reassessment trigger without claiming a universal safe speed or that the speed limit guarantees safety. A more cautious feasible choice is acceptable if explained. |
| Reassess and choose alternatives | Uses the supplied safe stop, checks route suitability and chooses a feasible next plan; explains why an unverified shortcut or arrival-driven choice is unsuitable. |
| Respect the continuation boundary | At the final point explicitly refuses to resume with the unfit driver; any replacement/transport choice meets supplied conditions. Slowing down, proximity or a different road cannot compensate for inability to drive safely. |
| Explain and reflect | Gives a reason for each action, rejects a tempting unsafe alternative, revises the arrival expectation and names a future reassessment trigger. Reflection identifies an early warning and the influence of pressure without treating cancellation as failure. |

**Reviewer acceptance of future authored content:** A model review must cover all three stages and explain each mistake. Two reviewers must independently agree on the final no-continuation boundary and on which earlier alternatives are defensible from supplied facts. Accept equivalent safe plans with sound reasoning; do not require a single route choice where facts support several. Reject a response that resumes unsafe driving even if its other checklist entries are correct. No hidden weather, vehicle, stopping-place or replacement-driver fact may determine acceptance.

## M10-L03 · Explain a whole journey — 9N3

**Status:** Brief complete, 19 October 2026; proposed content requires a separate content decision before authoring or integration.

### Input and objective

Use the [M10-L03 objective, scenario and reflection](../manuscript.md#m10-l03--explain-a-whole-journey), C084 in the [evidence register](../manuscript-evidence.md#c084--m10-l03), and the complete [9B17 import](implementation-evidence/9B17/README.md). The [7D gap carried into the backlog](implementation-backlog.md#9n--close-assessment-coverage-briefs-without-changing-the-baseline-bank) records **zero primary and zero related questions**. Retain the existing scenario and knowledge check. Their presence as teaching does not establish bank sampling or independently assessed whole-journey reasoning.

Assess whether the learner can review a mixed outward/return journey, justify decisions from the facts and relevant teaching, identify missed hazards and propose specific safer alternatives. The response must examine the decision process, including an apparently successful outcome that depended on luck, and finish with an actionable reflection.

### Sources and rationale rechecked 19 October 2026

The lesson is an original integrative exercise, not an official test question or a verified court case. Its principal source is the [Traffic Regulation, chapters 2–3](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/trafikforordning-19981276_sfs-1998-1276/), read alongside the explanations in M02–M07. Relevant locators include chapter 2 §1 (care), chapter 3 §§2 and 14 (gap, speed/control), §22 (roundabout entry), §§32 and 36 (safe overtaking and clear path), and §§50 and 53(1) (safe exit and stopping restrictions at/before crossings). Rechecked these provisions for this brief; future authors must recheck them and any additional rules needed by their exact scene.

The original scenario leaves some facts unspecified. A space described merely as “before a crossing” is not enough to decide whether it falls within the ten-metre restriction. The future exercise must state distance, travel direction and applicable restrictions. Likewise, a bend needs an explicit visibility/path description; do not imply that every bend creates an identical prohibition. This brief turns the existing teaching into an assessable proposal by requiring evidence, linked rationale and reflection across all stages, rather than forcing one MCQ. It does not refresh frozen source dates or certify learner readiness to drive.

### Proposed format and supplied input

Propose one **unscored, text-based journey review and reflection**, using the existing practice/reflection approach. This is a content brief, without a new screen, artwork, recording feature or stored learner history. No account, personal journey or real driving performance is required.

A future author supplies a fictional journey record based on the existing rural-appointment scenario: unfamiliar car, passenger, residential street, roundabout, faster road, later rain, destination stop and delayed return with pressure to overtake. Include a chronological account of the driver's actual choices, observable hazards and outcomes. Include at least one explicitly supported missed hazard and one safe decision; do not let “find the mistake” imply every action was wrong. Supply these details:

- Predeparture fitness/entitlement and vehicle/passenger readiness facts, unfamiliar controls, route and fallback information; mark any unresolved fact explicitly.
- Residential signs/speed, sight obstructions and crossing users; roundabout layout, lane markings, destination and other traffic. Do not require guessing a lane or priority from an absent diagram.
- Faster-road observation/gap and changes in rain/visibility, with enough facts to explain adaptation without an invented exact safe speed.
- A proposed destination space explicitly within ten metres before a pedestrian crossing in the direction of travel, ordinary conditions with no applicable exception, plus a lawful feasible alternative and passenger-exit conditions.
- A return overtaking proposal whose full clear path cannot be established, pressure/delay facts and a feasible wait/replan alternative. Include one no-collision outcome whose record explicitly shows reliance on another road user's avoidance or an unjustified margin.

The learner returns a five-stage review with **observed fact / decision and rationale / missed hazard or remaining uncertainty / safer alternative**, then a short reflection stating **trigger noticed or missed / influence on the decision / specific action to practise next time**. Accept concise bullets or prose; assess reasoning rather than writing length. Cite the relevant lesson or rule explanation; memorised provision numbers are not required.

### Required coverage and plausible mistakes

| Journey stage | Evidence the response must explain | Plausible mistake to diagnose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before departure | Resolve the supplied readiness/controls/route issues before setting off; explain why each matters | Assume entitlement alone proves fitness or that familiarity with another car removes the need to locate controls |
| Residential area and roundabout | Anticipate the supplied hidden/crossing hazards and explain entry, lane and exit decisions separately using the stated layout | Treat a clear immediate view as proof no one can emerge, or treat entry priority as permission for any lane change/exit |
| Faster road and rain | Explain how observation, gap, speed and lighting need reassessment as conditions change | Treat the posted limit or unchanged vehicle capability as proof the original margins remain adequate |
| Destination | Reject the explicitly restricted space and describe the feasible lawful stop/manoeuvre and safe passenger exit | Choose convenience or a quick drop-off as an exemption from a stopping restriction |
| Delayed return | Reject overtaking without a complete clear path; manage pressure and change arrival expectations | Treat a slow lead vehicle, urgency or the absence of a collision as evidence an unjustified manoeuvre was sound |

### Checkable acceptance rubric

Mark each criterion **met / needs revision**, citing a passage in the learner's review. All six must be met to demonstrate the objective. This is a review rubric, not an app score, official test threshold or permission to drive.

| Criterion | Observable evidence needed for “met” |
| --- | --- |
| Whole-journey coverage | Addresses all five stages, including predeparture and return; explains at least one sound decision as well as a missed hazard. |
| Evidence and rationale | Connects each decision to a supplied fact and the relevant teaching/rule; separates observation from assumption and flags unresolved information rather than inventing it. |
| Hazard recognition | Identifies the deliberately supplied missed hazard and explains who could be harmed/how the margin was lost; also reassesses the changed rain/visibility conditions. |
| Safe alternatives | Gives a feasible action at every identified unsafe decision, including a lawful destination stop and refusal of the unjustified overtaking proposal. Does not trade away stopping, priority or control duties for convenience. |
| Process versus outcome | Explains why the specified no-collision outcome does not validate the decision, identifying the actual dependence on luck/avoidance or an unjustified margin from the record. |
| Actionable reflection | Names a specific trigger and the pressure/attention issue, then states an observable next-time action, such as voicing the decision to wait before considering a pass. “Be more careful” alone is insufficient. |

**Reviewer acceptance of future authored content:** Provide an explained model review for all five stages, linked teaching references and feedback for each listed mistake. Two reviewers must independently identify the deliberately unsafe stop, unjustified overtake and missed hazard from the supplied facts. Accept alternative lawful, feasible plans with evidence-based reasoning. An endorsed prohibited stop or unsafe overtake prevents acceptance regardless of other strengths. Do not require a collision to recognise failure, reject a safe decision solely because of a later outcome, or infer actual driving competence from a written reflection. Facts must determine the decisions without hidden geometry, traffic rules or vehicle assumptions.

# KörkortGo illustration instructions

**Added:** 2 October 2026, at the user's request. Applies to the book, lesson material, self-assessment and later app illustrations. Read together with the [design guide](README.md).

## Teaching coverage

Illustrations are part of the teaching material, not just cover decoration. Add relevant visuals to lessons and especially to worked scenarios, knowledge checks and module self-assessment. Show the situation the learner must interpret: road layout, vehicle paths, people, sight lines, signs or changing conditions. Use an illustration where spatial relationships or observation are important; do not fill every page with decorative images.

Before producing the illustrated book, audit all 40 lessons and 10 module reviews. Record each required scene or sign example, its learning purpose, exact manuscript anchor, caption, alternative text, source evidence and approval status. If a scenario does not benefit from an illustration, record the editorial reason. Extend the existing D01–D08 briefs where needed rather than assuming those eight cover every scenario.

For a knowledge check or self-assessment, place the neutral situation image before the question and keep the answer or explanatory overlay separate. Do not reveal the answer through a highlighted vehicle, route arrow or caption unless that information is intentionally part of the question. Multiple-stage scenarios may need a labelled sequence rather than one confusing composite junction.

## Consistent Swedish setting

Use one coherent editorial illustration style based on the [learn-and-prepare](../mockups/01-learn-and-prepare.png) and [practise-and-review](../mockups/02-practise-and-review.png) mockups: clear silhouettes, restrained natural colours, readable road geometry and limited visual clutter. The brand palette governs the surrounding page; instructional objects retain their correct colours.

- **Vehicles:** Use a consistent family of everyday passenger cars, cycles, buses and goods vehicles appropriate to Swedish roads. Preserve proportions and recognisable vehicle colours across a scenario sequence. Show right-hand traffic and steering/control position appropriate to the actual example. Avoid unrelated US-style road imagery or decorative brand logos.
- **Roads:** Use Swedish road markings, junction geometry, crossings, verges, barriers and sign placement appropriate to each verified example. Distinguish urban, rural, motorway and winter scenes. Show a centre line only when it belongs in that scene; never import another country's markings merely for visual familiarity.
- **Police:** Base uniforms, high-visibility garments, the “POLIS” label, vehicles and directed gestures on current official Swedish references. Do not invent insignia or substitute foreign uniforms. A gesture that teaches an instruction must be checked for its exact meaning; generated artwork alone is not evidence.
- **People:** Depict a varied population of road users in realistic clothing for Swedish weather and the situation. Swedish context comes from the setting, equipment and behaviour, not a single ethnicity or appearance. Include pedestrians, cyclists, passengers and mobility aids where relevant to the lesson.
- **Environment:** Use credible Swedish streets, settlements, forests, lakes and seasonal conditions. Details should support the learning objective rather than obscure a road user or hazard.

Create a small reference sheet for recurring cars, people, police and road treatments before producing the full set. Record approved reference images and their provenance; reuse them as imagegen references so characters, vehicles, perspective and line treatment remain consistent. Official reference photos used for factual checking are not automatically licensed for reproduction; record reuse status for any source asset actually incorporated.

## Signs and factual accuracy

Use the existing verified Transportstyrelsen asset/caption bindings for official signs, plates, signals and markings. Do not redraw or generate substitute official signs. A generated sign in a concept image is only a placeholder until replaced by a verified asset. Keep every held asset excluded; the 13 selected image gaps remain open until the sign-package process resolves them.

Check each instructional scene against the manuscript and official evidence: applicable rule, direction of travel, lane positions, vehicle paths, viewing angle, pedestrian/cyclist position, sight obstruction and relevant sign/plate combination. Do not turn separate catalogue examples into an apparently lawful roadside assembly without reviewing their combined meaning. Retain the existing restrictions on exact parking composites, crossing identifiers, equipment images and moving/audible instructions.

Keep factual reference URLs, checking dates and asset permissions in the illustration register. Link captions to the relevant lesson and official reference. Distinguish an original teaching illustration from official Transportstyrelsen artwork, and never imply agency endorsement.

## Required workflow before book production

1. Revise the 5D lesson/self-assessment previews to include representative scenario illustrations and sign placement, following these requirements. The first 5D boards remain historical layout drafts and are not approved as the final illustrated treatment.
2. In 5E, complete the illustration coverage register and consistent-style reference sheet. Preview the required diagrams and sign layouts with imagegen, using verified factual references and approved style references.
3. Review factual meaning, visual consistency, captions, alternative text and whether an assessment image prematurely reveals the answer. Save any corrections.
4. Obtain explicit user approval of revised visual previews before implementation. Revisions require renewed approval. Only approval of a particular preview counts; recording these instructions does not approve artwork.
5. In 5F, confirm every required illustration is approved and all asset blockers are resolved. Carry exact file/caption/source bindings into the design specification.
6. In step 6, build the illustrated book using the full manuscript and approved assets. Inspect actual-size readability, image resolution, sign details, reading order, alternative text and references. Do not leave internal placeholders or approval notes in the learner-facing book.

This update records requirements before production; it does not generate new artwork, resolve sign holds or start implementation.
